## Chemistry

Four elements A–D: masses 0.5–2, valences 1–4 and an affinity parameter are generated from the seed. Values are arbitrary units of an artificial world. Formulas are assembled by adding previously created formulas; up to six atoms per molecule. Duplicate formulas are not allowed. Each formula has two energy states; this gives 32 species, compatible with the operands of the existing VM.

`Atoms[s,e]` is the canonical composition. `Mass[s] = Σ Atoms[s,e] × AtomicMass[e]`. Cohesion and packing are derived from element parameters and formula complexity. Hardness and reactivity arise from properties rather than being assigned to rock names. Names, colours, energies and physical parameters are reproducible within a given generator version.

- `Combine[a,b] == -1`: no reaction. Otherwise the sum of input atoms equals the atoms of the product.
- `SplitA[s] == -1`: no decomposition. `SplitB[s] == -1` for a single product, e.g. relaxation of an excited monomer.
- `PhotoUp[s]` conserves composition and raises energy. The reaction consumes a whole available photon; a fractional probability does not grant free full excitations.
- The energy of an endothermic reaction is debited from the body; an exothermic reaction gives the difference to the body and to heat. The heat share (`HeatShare`, 30%) first warms the body (`Tb`) and accumulates in `Agent.HeatHeld`, and goes into cells as the body cools — at the same rate at which `Tb` follows the surroundings. This way it is counted once. Harm from cold and heat grows exponentially, but the exponent is capped by `HarmExpMax` (20): harm is finite at any `Tb`.
- A reaction without a matching protein stays slow; a protein changes the rate, not the stoichiometry.

The ready-made ladder "clay cofactor → granite → ore → diamond" no longer exists. `MatTier` is only a computed barrier category for statistics. The only special identifiers are: 0 — void, 1 — the world's bottom boundary. `s + 2` is the aggregate prototype of molecule s.

## Rock representation

Dense arrays are indexed by `v = cell * Z + z`:

- `Mat[v]`: prototype / dominant molecule for rendering and as a catalysis target;
- `Units[v]`: how many molecules remain in the block;
- `Order[v]`: structural order 0…255;
- `Pressure[v]`: load from the last computation;
- `Height[cell]`: the first level above the topmost non-empty block. **This is not the number of solid blocks.**

A block is counted in molecules and is full when their volume fills the voxel (`Chemistry.MatCap[m]` = `VoxelSpace / Volume` — from a hundred to thousands of molecules; `Units` is a `ushort`). An unmodified block is a single species (`Mat − 2`), `Units` of them. Built and mixed blocks use `mixtures[v]`: an array of actual molecules, with the prefix of length `Units[v]` active. After partial mining it must not be replaced with `MatSeq[Mat[v]]`: that would lose the mixture's composition.

`Buried[v]` is additional matter at depth, with fractional quantities, pressure and order. It is a buried body, a crushed aggregate or adsorbed atmospheric material. Its mass loads the block; its ordering affects its contribution to cohesion. When a block moves, the burial moves with it. When a block is removed, the remains sink to the nearest floor below. Matter available on an open floor can be taken up again.

`PutMixture`, `TakeVoxelMolecule`, `RemoveVoxel`, `TransferVoxel`, `MatterChanged` maintain geometry/strength invalidation. `TerrainChanged(cell)` also bumps the version of that particular column for the view. After changing `Buried.Matter`, `MatterChanged(v)` is required; otherwise the mass/cohesion cache may go stale. The arrays are public for reading by the view and diagnostics, not for arbitrary writes.

## Strength and pressure

Units are arbitrary. Parameters live in `P.cs`; in particular `Gravity`, `CompressionK`, `TensionK`, `CompactionPressure`, `StructureEvery`, `MetamorphEvery`.

Block weight: mass of its molecules and burials × gravity, plus a share of the weight of creatures on the floor above it (in water — submerged weight, `mass − ρwater·volume`, not below zero). A creature's mass includes whole molecules, `Pend` and protein substrate. A large body spreads its weight across the cells it occupies.

Compressive strength grows with cohesion and order, and under partial mining is multiplied by the square of the remaining cross-section fraction. Lateral/tensile bonding additionally depends on how well compositions match, the order of both sides and the weakest material. Identical pure aggregates bond more strongly; different states even of the same formula may have a weaker joint. Contact between mixed blocks is computed from their real molecules, not just the dominant colour.

The computation runs once every `StructureEvery` ticks:

1. Settle bodies onto accessible floors; collect their weights and determine changed loads.
2. Recompute affected columns: the solid part from the bottom boundary is grounded, blocks above voids are overhanging.
3. From grounded contacts, propagate the maximum residual support margin through a priority queue. The chosen parent has a larger margin: no self-supporting cycle can arise. Every reachable block gets a path, even if its margin is negative (the support will not hold it): its weight must rest somewhere, and the overload shows up as a break along that path.
4. In reverse order, sum the weight of branches. Blocks sharing one bond or support sum their loads. Lateral load is also passed down through grounded support.
5. Bond failure of a block: if there is a void beneath it, the block falls to the floor. If it rests on another overhanging block, failure merely means its weight goes downward: the load is added to the path of the lower block, and wherever that path cannot hold it, the failure moves there (and further down). A stack that is too heavy collapses at its weakest point instead of standing forever with its weight going "nowhere".
6. **Compressive strength with lateral confinement (Mohr–Coulomb, `Strength`).** A block holds its uniaxial strength (`CompressionCapacity`: cohesion × (`LooseStrength` + (1.5 − `LooseStrength`) × order²) × fill²; intrinsic strength is the cement between grains, it grows with order, and for disordered mass — a heap, rubble — it is nearly zero, `LooseStrength` = 0.001) plus `FrictionQ` × the minimum lateral stress σ₃ with which its neighbours confine it. σ₃ comes from a small elastic problem on the row of blocks through the block at its level, up to 4 blocks each way along each horizontal axis (`Confinement`, solved exactly as a tridiagonal system): each block would press sideways with `LateralK` × its vertical stress if it could not move (at rest), it is a spring as stiff as it is strong (stiffness ∝ uniaxial strength + friction under that confinement; the common modulus ratio cancels), and it is held in place by shear against the blocks above and below (shear modulus from the Poisson ratio ν = K/(1 + K) that gives `LateralK`). Two neighbours meet through their contact — the same rock fully (1), a joint between dissimilar rocks poorly (0.2–0.4), a partially mined block by its fill — and the stress across the face is continuous; a void is a free face (zero stress), bedrock, the end of the map and the far end of the row hold their side still. Stress along an axis is the lesser of the two faces; the weaker axis decides. So a free face is unconfined and the block behind it only partly, the at-rest stress comes back over one or two blocks, and a block between a tall and a low column gets a stress between theirs (before, a neighbour pushed with its own at-rest stress whatever stood behind it). Approximation: the shear coupling holds each block against layers taken as still, so the relief zone is a block or two wide whatever the height of the face (a tall cliff relieves deeper in nature). Hence rock inside a stratum holds the overburden above it, while a cliff edge, a free-standing pillar, a tunnel wall and a free heap hold only their uniaxial strength; a stratum of one rock is stronger than a stack of heterogeneous layers. `FrictionQ` ≈ 4 (friction angle ~37°) and `LateralK` ≈ 0.4 (at-rest earth pressure) are laws of mechanics, not rock properties: properties come from molecules. The solver wakes columns using the earlier conservative estimate without confinement; the decision to fail is made by `Strength`.
   A failed lattice stays as **rubble in place** (`CrushToRubble`, `Order` = 0): the same molecules, the same volume, less strength. Matter does not go into burials (previously a crushed block became a burial — mass without volume — and a heavy heap would cascade, "sucking" the whole column down into lower voxels). Rubble that does not hold its load behaves as a granular mass (`FlowRubble`): it flows only downward — into the pores of the block below, into a neighbouring voxel one level lower (empty or with room), or level with itself but only over an edge where there is a void below (it will fall further); it does not flow sideways over flat ground (otherwise a heap would shuffle back and forth). "There is room" if at least 2% of the voxel is free (`HasRoom`, more than any molecule). A lattice fails only if the crushed mass has somewhere to dilate into (`FitOrder`, `CanSwell`): a block confined on all sides does not fail. A voxel is freed only if everything has left. A heap spreads until its slopes are confined or hold their load: the angle of repose emerges from strength and confinement rather than being prescribed. Metamorphism orders only blocks that hold their load. Regressions `rubble`, `confinement`.
7. **Porosity and compaction (`PackedVolume`).** A molecule in a block occupies `Volume × (1 + Bulking × looseness × (1 − order))`; looseness is derived from the molecule's packing (`Chemistry.Looseness` = 1.15 − `Packing`): irregular, poorly packing molecules bulk more. Disordered mass takes up to (1 + `Bulking`) times more space than the same molecules in a lattice (`Bulking` = 0.4; in nature crushed rock "bulks" by a factor of 1.3–1.5). Hence:
   - **generation** puts into a voxel as many molecules as fit at its order (`BlockCapacity`): upper disordered strata are lighter, deep ordered ones denser; density increases with depth;
   - **crushing and impact on falling cause bulking** (dilatancy, `Dilate`): a failed lattice no longer fits in its voxel, the excess is squeezed upward or toward an open side as new rubble; where it is compressed on all sides and no room can be found, the lattice stays as intact as needed to fit;
   - **pressure compacts** (`Metamorphose`): an ordering step requires pressure `CompactionPressure × e^(DensifyK × order)` (`DensifyK` = 3), so each further compaction step needs exponentially greater pressure, and a dense lattice barely compacts further. Space freed up is filled by settling rubble and deposits;
   - deposits, rubble flow and volcanic output compute available room taking the block's order into account.
8. **Elastic strain, settling and rebound (`World.Settle.cs`).** A block under vertical stress σ is squeezed by ε = σ / E, its stiffness E = `ModulusRatio` × its uniaxial strength (E/UCS ≈ 100–500 for rock, default 300): stiffness comes from the same bonds, order and contacts as strength, and a block that bears its load is strained at most 1/`ModulusRatio` (the strain at which rock fails). A squeezed block holds `VoxelSpace`/(1 − ε). Each column sums what packs its grounded stack — the room compaction frees (`Metamorphose`) and the change of elastic room as its load changes — and, since sub-voxel shifts are below the grid's resolution, acts when the sum reaches a whole voxel: the highest porous block (room ≥ 2% by its squeezed space) gives its molecules to the other porous blocks, nearest first, and everything above it in the grounded stack comes down one level (whole voxels: strata keep their makeup; the sub-voxel shifts of the full blocks in between are rounded away). Unloading by a voxel's worth of squeezed room springs the column back: everything above the highest over-full block rises one level and the new voxel takes the excess of the over-full blocks. Nothing happens unless the room (or excess) is really there; room left by gnawing never makes a column settle. The world's rock is born in equilibrium (the first look only records its strain). The weight coming down × one level goes to heat as an impact (outside the chemical ledger). At the default stiffness rock under its own weight strains ~0.01–0.1% (pressure ~100 against strength 500–2000), so elastic settling of a whole voxel needs loads far beyond what the world builds; what settles columns in practice is compaction of loose masses (heaps, rubble, deposits). Regression `settle` (with a soft law, `ModulusRatio` 2: a loaded column comes down, unloaded it springs back; a compacting loose stratum lets its column settle).

**A falling block's blow (`Crush`).** Bodies in its path share the impact (the block's weight × (1 + fall)) by the room they take — the rest goes into the ground — and each is torn by its share as far as its molecules hold: tearing a molecule out costs the same work as gnawing it from a disordered lump of those molecules (`FaceWork` · e^(barrier − `FaceBarrier`), barrier from the molecule's bond at order 0). A body of strongly bonded molecules (a shell) loses less to the same blow, a big one has more to lose; torn molecules lie buried where the block landed, and a body left with fewer than `MinBody` molecules dies (`CauseBuried`, its constituents in `Buried` at the impact site). Survivors where the block landed are pushed aside or onto it. Under the world's gravity a block falling a few levels carries ~2–10 units of energy against ~5 per loosely bonded molecule, so a fall scratches a body rather than killing it outright (before, any body in the path died). Regression `impact`. An agent standing **above** a falling block is not considered to be under it, but falling along with its support may harm it. The VM immediately stops commands when the executing body dies, including inside `dig`.

**Repose and placement.** A top block placed on a column (sediment, `grow`, `pile`, `dig`, volcano) goes through `Repose`: if a neighbour is lower than the allowed step (the same computation as slope creep in `Erode` — cohesion × order), the block slides there, lifting creatures standing there. Sediment immediately settles into the lowest neighbouring cell. `DropVoxel`: transfer no more than one level down is a soft placement; only a fall from a height of two levels or more kills. `dig` (digging, not eating) moves a whole block to a neighbouring floor: at its own level or one lower, and if there are none — upward, to the lowest edge (a spoil heap around the pit). Each level of lifting costs `VoxelMass·Gravity`; dropping from more than one level is not allowed. This is how shafts and burrows are dug; eating from rock (`mine`) remains slow.

**Mining is a process, not a lucky blow** (`Mine`, `TakeBite`). Each attempt costs effort: the more, the harder and higher-class the rock. Hard molecules in the body ("teeth", carapace) take on part of this work. All invested work (`effort / FaceWork`) stays in the face and accumulates. A molecule comes out when the face has accumulated `e^(barrier·(1−catalysis) − FaceBarrier)` (`FaceBarrier` = 1). Progress is stored in fractions of a molecule of that face (`Bite[c]`): work done at one barrier is not spent at another. A face is one voxel: if work moves to another level of the column or the block there has changed (a different `Mat`), progress starts over, so loosening the surface does not pay for someone gnawing deep below. The barrier `barrier = RockBarrier·(0.15 + cohesion²)·(0.35 + order)` combines bond strength, lattice order and how poorly the molecules of a mixture fit together (`TypicalBarrier(m, order)` — the same for panels). Time also slowly loosens an exposed face: `BiteRate/e^(barrier−1)` molecules per tick, up to `BiteCap` molecules in reserve (weathering); a new face starts from zero.
- **Reach.** `mine` and `dig` in a neighbouring column take the block level with the body or the top of the column no more than one step down (as far as the body can step); the bottom of a pit is out of reach.
- **Several gnawers add their work.** Five at one face progress five times faster. Even the strongest monolith eventually gives way.
- **The limit is set by energy, not by a cap.** A molecule costs about `FaceWork·e^(barrier − 1)` effort (without teeth; `FaceWork` = 9):
  - loose deposits, body excretions and mixtures (barrier 0.3–0.6) — 4.5–6 effort, roughly as much energy as an average molecule holds. Rock is primarily a material for growth, and it yields energy only where the molecule is rich;
  - an ordered monolith of strong bonds (barrier 4–5) — 180–490 effort;
  - deep compressed rock (barrier 15–40) — practically infinite.

  At `FaceWork` = 3 loose rock paid off threefold, and worlds exploded to 65–90 thousand creatures.
- **A protein for the bond** removes up to 92% of the barrier, and hard rock starts to cost as much as loose rock.

The "Matter" panel shows, for each aggregate, the cost of a molecule in effort without and with a protein; it is convenient to compare with the molecule's energy E.

**Deposits.** Excretions (`grow`), constructions (`pile`), compacted remains (`Settle`) do not create a block from a handful of molecules: `Deposit` tops up the block under the floor until it is full by volume, and only the surplus starts a new block on top (creatures standing there are pushed aside or lifted, the builder stands on it). A new block takes no more than one voxel by volume; the rest lies loose on it (there are no overfull blocks; `PutMixture` checks that the molecule count does not exceed `ushort`). If there is nowhere to put it, the matter stays loose — nothing is lost. Repose (`Repose`) is checked only if the block landed on top of a column; a deposit on a cave floor does not shift the top of the column.

Once every `MetamorphEvery` ticks, pressure raises the order of rock. For burials, the rate also depends on temperature; allowed exothermic combinations conserve elements. No ready-made material such as coal is assigned.

## Bodies and cavities

`Agent.Z` is the free level above the current floor. `WalkLevel` checks cell accessibility, climbing one block and dropping to a lower floor. **Ledges higher than a block** (`Move`, `LedgeLevel`, `Lifted`): a body gets onto one by its own momentum, a block of rise per unit of speed (what one push gives along the ground; one block up still needs only the step itself). The momentum is what it carries this step plus what it has been pressing against that ledge (`Agent.Climb`, `ClimbDir`): kept while it keeps pushing the same way and bled by ground friction like motion, so a strong push or several pushes in one tick get it up at once, a steady full push for a few ticks gets it up about six blocks and no more, a weak motor less. Its own column must be open up to the ledge's top (it climbs up the face, not through its roof). Being carried to another cell by a current (`Drift`) ends the pressing. The work against gravity is `CostClimb` × rise × mass, as for one block; each push already paid `CostPush` × mass. Regression `climb`. `Place` manages only the horizontal list; the calling code must set Z correctly. An agent's height must not be computed from `Height`.

Offspring are born at a level reachable from the parent. Social actions check vertical proximity; creatures on the surface and deep beneath it are not neighbours merely because of equal X/Y. Uptake, excretion and death use the accessible level. Excretion into a neighbouring cell lands on its reachable floor (level, one step higher, or down into a pit), and if there is a wall there — at the body's feet; not inside rock and not into the cave's air. An underground body cannot take up surface food or photons through the vault; an exposed part of a large body can receive light.

A protein contains `Material` and `Matter`, not only catalytic activity `Amount`. Expression moves substrate from the free inventory into the protein without changing mass. Wear and damage return it through a fractional reservoir. When a protein is replaced and on death, its matter is also returned.

## Bodies against bodies (`World.Predation.cs`, `World.Vm.cs`)

A living body is a disordered aggregate of the molecules it holds, and it holds them as a face of the same aggregate holds its molecules. Nothing about predators, prey or parasites is built in; costs and gains come from the same quantities as mining.

- **Hold.** `BodyCohesion` is the cohesion of a mixed block of the body's molecules (`CoreCohesion`: mean bond × how well they fit, `Chemistry.Contact`), with no lattice order. The barrier of pulling one molecule out is that of a face of this aggregate, `RockBarrier·(0.15 + c²)·0.35`, lowered by the puller's split protein for the body's dominant molecule (`Catalysis`, as in mining); the work per molecule is `TearWork = BodyHold·FaceWork·e^(barrier − FaceBarrier)` (the law of `TakeBite`; `BodyHold` = 1). A body of strong, well-fitting molecules is a shell: in the test (`--self-test-predation`) six strikes of 20 tear 22 molecules out of 0.47-bond bodies and none out of 4.45-bond ones. A shell is not free: strong molecules are heavier (over the seed's molecules ρ(bond, mass) ≈ 0.6 in seed 2), and mass costs `CostMass` upkeep, climbing and pushing.
- **`attack`.** The strike is work `argument × StrikeUnit` (0.1), at most the store − 1, plus `CostSocial`. Its work tears molecules out at `TearWork` each (stochastic rounding of the last, never more than was paid) — that work is heat in the cell — and the rest of the strike goes into the victim as heat: from the attacker's store into the victim's `HeatHeld` (both in the energy stock, no flow), warming it by `6/(5 + mass)` per unit like reaction heat. A hot body's proteins unfold and it pays heat harm through the existing temperature law; a big body warms less. There is no other injury term and no "strength" bonus. A body with fewer than `MinBody` molecules or no energy dies (`CauseKilled`).
- **Store with matter.** Molecules torn or pulled out carry `TornStore` (1) of their share of the victim's store (`store × molecules taken / molecules it had`) to the taker (`CarryStore`, a transfer between bodies; the doubles' rounding is booked as Rounding). `give` and `expel` do not: the body lets go of a molecule it has unloaded.
- **`take`.** Pulling one molecule of a kind costs `TearWork` (paid whether or not the partner lets go; the taker needs the work + 1), and an unlinked partner keeps it with chance by mass `1 − (m+1)/(m+M+2)`; a linked partner does not resist.
- **`inject`** succeeds with the same mass chance as `take` (it had a ×1.5 before).
- **Signals** live `AlarmTicks` (16, `hurt`) and `HandshakeTicks` (8, mate and link); `share` hands over `argument × ShareUnit` (1/8); mating takes `MateShare` (¼) of each parent's energy and molecules. These were literals and are now laws with the same values.
- **Parasites are followed** (observation, saved since version 13): a pushed `inject` into another lineage stores the written piece (≥ 8 bytes) as `Agent.Foreign` with its origin `ForeignFrom`; a child inherits it when the piece is still whole in its genome (`InheritForeign`; counter `ParasiteInherited`); a host that copies a piece containing it into another body with its own `inject` spreads it (`ParasiteSpreads`, chronicle event `ParasiteSpread` once, important); a `cut` that removes the piece clears it. `FirstParasite` now needs the child to really carry the piece (before: any child of a parent that had once been infected).
- **Predation probe** (`World.PredProbe`, bench `--predation`): attacks, work, molecules torn and their bond and split energy, shares from close kin (Kinship ≥ 54) and from the own lineage, kills and their remains (and how much of them was taken in again in the same cells — an upper bound of scavenging), thefts, the energy into bodies by source (photons, environment molecules, bodies, carried stores), defence by the victim's cohesion, parasites, and territory (individual crowding in 3×3 and regions of 16×16: deaths, children and growth against density). `--osc runs.csv` tests a batch for Lotka–Volterra cycles of the hunter share (`Oscillation`): detrended share over binomial noise (`share_osc`), and hunters following prey (lagged correlation of detrended log counts) against circular-shift surrogates. Counting only: the state hash is the same with the probe.
- Approximations: the hold is per body, not per molecule; all cells of a big body hold alike; a strike's heat goes into the whole victim at once; remains on the floor are loose matter (eating them is intake); the kin threshold is the metrics' `KinRadius`.

## Life models (`LifeModels.cs`, `World.BodyOps.cs`)

How a body decides what to do is a **life model** (`ILifeModel`), and several can live side by side in one world. Model 1 (`LifeModels.Vm` = 1, `VmModel`) is the genome as a stack-machine program (`World.Vm.cs`); a second model (a cell as a reaction network with receptors, signals and motors made of proteins) is being designed separately. Every model shares the same physics: a body of any model absorbs, excretes, eats and is eaten, dies into the soil, is saved, shown, planted and copied the same way.

- **Shared, physical:** the body's matter (`Inv`, `Pend`, proteins `Enz` with their substrate), energy (`Energy`, `HeatHeld`) and the ledger, volume and mass, place (`X`, `Y`, `Z`, `Lift`, footprint), temperature, links, the target it attends to (`Target`), the signal others hear (`Signal`), upkeep (including `CostLen` per genome byte), wear, UV point hits on the genome, death and burial.
- **Model-specific, opaque to the world:** what the genome bytes mean (`Agent.G`, with `Agent.Prot` — how much each byte has proven useful, raised by `Worked` at the `site` a primitive was called with), how they are inherited (`RandomGenome`, `Mutate`, `Cross`, `InheritState`), the controller state between ticks (model 1: `Ip`, stack, memory, calls in the base body record; another model: `Agent.ModelState`, saved by its `SyncState`), the text form for designs and the UI (`Describe`, `TryCompile`), its example designs (`DefaultDesigns`).
- **Body physics** (`World.BodyOps.cs`; `Divide`, `Mate` in `World.Life.cs`): the only way a controller acts. Each primitive books its own physical cost, so the same act costs the same whichever model asked for it: membrane `Intake`/`Drink`/`Expel`; reactions with held proteins `MakeProtein`, `Bind`, `SplitMol`, `DigestAny`, `Photo`; force `Motor` (push, swim, climb via movement); contact `PickBody`, `Partner`, `Attack`, `Take`, `Give`, `Share`, `LinkOp`, `Inject`; genome `Cut`, `Divide`, `Mate`; rock `Mine`, `Dig`, `Pile`, `Grow`; own work `Spend`. Reading local quantities is free (what a receptor could sense): `OutsideAmount`, `NeighbourCell`, `AmbientTemp`, `LightAt`, `PhotonsAt`, `UvSense`, `Gradient`, `GroundAt`, `ContactCount`, `Alarm`, `Look` (this one costs `CostLook` per cell, as before), `Kinship`, and the body's own fields. Model 1's opcodes are thin calls into these (same order of costs and random draws: the default world's hash is unchanged by the split).
- **Dispatch.** `Agent.Model` (byte, default 1) selects the model; `LiveBody` calls `LifeModels.Get(a.Model).Think(world, a, cell)` once per body per tick in the creature phase, before movement, links, temperature and upkeep. `Think` is under the same thread contract as everything in that phase ("Performance and threads"): act only through the body physics, random numbers only from `world.Rng`, no per-call allocation in the common path. A child is of its parent's model; `Mate` needs both partners of the same model; `Inject` into a body of another model is paid for and refused. `SetGenome` calls the model's `GenomeChanged` (model 1: labels); splices call `SpliceSite`/`BeforeSplice` (model 1: inserted at `Ip`, a cut moves `Ip` back and forgets the call stack).
- **Files.** Saves from version 14 end every body record with the model id and the model's own state (`SyncState`, symmetric; model 1 has none); older files load as model 1, and writing an older version refuses bodies of another model. Design (`CreatureDesign.Model`) and population (`PopulationBody.Model`) files carry the model's text key, absent for model 1 (files of model 1 are as before); a population's protein-gene remapping between chemistries applies to model 1 only. Regions copy bodies through the same body record.
- **Not yet model-aware** (model 1 everywhere): abiogenesis (`SpawnAt` draws `Genome.Random`), the census of instructions (`OpCount`, `--ops`), the inspector, fossils and the chronicle's genome text (disassembled as model 1), the course of evolution's genome components (they hash genome bytes of any model alike).
- Verified: seeds 1 and 3 give the same state hashes at 1000/2000/3000 ticks before and after; `--self-test` passes, with a test model living beside model 1 (`LifeModelRegression`: atoms exact, the ledger closes, no cross-model mating or injection, save/load v14 keeps model and state); a version 13 file loads as the same world. How to add a model: [LIFE-MODELS.md](LIFE-MODELS.md) (Russian).

## Volume

The third base quantity after mass and energy (`World.Volume.cs`).
- **A molecule** occupies `Chemistry.Volume[s] = mass / (0.5 + packing)`: tightly packing molecules are more compact.
- **A body** occupies the sum of its molecules' volumes, including fractional uptake and protein matter (`Agent.Volume` is maintained together with mass; a regression checks it). A large body divides its volume among its cells.
- **Loose remains on the floor** occupy `LooseBulk` (2) times more than the same matter in a body. For the surface this is `LooseVolume[cell]`, updated together with soil chemistry.
- **Space above a floor** holds `VoxelSpace` (1200) of volume — bodies and remains together. The number of creatures is not limited: hundreds of tiny ones fit where dozens of ordinary ones or one giant fit.

Rules:
- **Entry and birth.** A body may enter a floor if it fits once everyone smaller than it has been pushed aside (`Fits`). A child, mating and spontaneous generation require room.
- **Relief.** After the creature phase, `Relieve` visits overfull floors. The smallest (ties broken by id) are shifted to neighbouring floors at the same level or lower, never upward, preferring the lowest. Shifts are first decided for all floors (room promised to arrivals is taken into account), then executed: a body shifted into a cell later in the traversal is not shifted a second time in the same tick, and a crowd does not drift in one direction. A fall during a shift of more than one block costs as an ordinary fall (`CostFall`); in water it does not injure. Stretched large bodies stay put. If there is nowhere to go, the crowd stays compressed — and heats up.
- **A block landing on an occupied floor** (sediment, `pile`, `grow`, creep, a lowered block): pushes those standing there aside or down, and if there is nowhere to go — lifts them by a block. A builder placing a block under itself stands on it. A large body that has an extra cell on that floor removes it (it will stretch again as it lives).
- **Remains beyond `CompactShare`** (30%) of a floor's volume on a real slope (a neighbour 2+ levels lower) flow downhill, and on gentle ground are compacted into an aggregate of their own molecules (`Settle`). The matter returns to the rock.

## Water

A body in a flooded column (`InWater`: depth ≥ `SwimDepth` over its floor — the column's surface water, or water standing on a cave floor, see «Water as a fluid» below) has, in addition to its floor `Z`, a height above the bottom `Agent.Lift` and a vertical velocity `Vz` (`World.Water.cs`). Depth and the highest reachable level come from `WaterOver`/`WaterTop` (in a cave: up to the top voxel under the roof).
- **Buoyancy.** Body density `Density = mass/volume` versus `WaterDensity` = 0.7. Water is lighter than any packed molecule (their density is 0.75–1.5), so **a body without gas sinks** and walks along the bottom. Gas (`Chem.Gas`) in a body is a bubble: it occupies `GasExpand` (8) packed volumes (`Chemistry.BodyVolume`), and a couple of such bubbles is enough to float up. Every tick `Vz += Buoyancy·(ρwater − ρ)/ρ`, then velocity is damped by drag `WaterDrag`; at the bottom and at the surface it is zeroed. The bubble also takes up room under the volume law.
- **Whether to swim at the top is decided by code.** Taking in gas (`intake` of gas near the surface, or gas from one's own decomposition reactions) — rise; expelling it (`expel`) or binding it into a compound (`bind`) — sink. The second way is a `swim` stroke, the second value of `nop`: variant 2 — up, 3 — down. It requires a motor protein, the stack is untouched, a stroke gives about a block. Out of water the command is empty and free, so movement on land is unchanged; `push` still pushes in 4 directions.
- **Cost of movement.** Water brakes harder than ground (`WaterFriction` 0.7 versus `Friction` 0.85). A step in water costs `CostSwim·mass·(1 + DepthK·depth below the surface)`, a motor push in water is `(1 + DepthK·depth)` times more expensive. Swimming near the surface is cheap, wading along the bottom of a deep lake is expensive. Climbing a step underwater costs according to submerged weight (`mass − ρwater·volume`); falls in water do not injure.
- **Height is preserved.** Moving to a neighbouring cell, a body stays at its absolute height as far as the water allows. A swimmer near the surface steps ashore level with the water. For one walking along the bottom, a lake wall higher than a block is a wall: it must rise.
- **Food by position** (`Exposure` in `Loose`). Lying on the bottom (`Lift < 0.5`), a body reaches remains there. Above the bottom only the dissolved fraction of remains is accessible to it, `Solubility·e^(−Lift)` = 1%·e^(−height above the bottom). Atmospheric gas dissolves from above: `e^(−depth below the surface)` is available, all of it right at the surface. A swimmer can gnaw rock (`mine`, `dig`, soaking an aggregate) only level with itself — it cannot reach the bottom. `ground 4` ("what is underfoot") returns 0 for a swimmer: there is water under it.
- **Light from above.** A cell's photons are counted at the water surface (`Photon` — from light without water above the bottom; `Light` — at the bottom, for temperature, vision and the view). Each photon reaches the body with probability `e^(−WaterDim·depth)`, the rest are absorbed by the water. There is most light near the surface and almost none at the bottom of a deep lake. UV damage to the genome is computed from light at the body.
- **Room.** A flooded floor holds `VoxelSpace·(1 + depth)`: the water above it is also space (a puddle shallower than `SwimDepth` is not). Swimmers are registered on their floor. A shift (`Relieve`, `Outlet`) preserves absolute height. A block landing under a swimmer above the bottom simply raises the bottom under it.
- **Neighbourhood.** `Near` and `look` compare absolute height `Z + Lift`. Creatures at the surface and at the bottom of a deep lake are not neighbours.

The result is a vertical trade-off, as in real water bodies:
- **Near the surface** there is light and gas but almost no matter. One can live there, but grow only on crumbs of dissolved matter: this is "micro-nutrition" for photosynthesisers that keep a bubble.
- **Near the bottom** there is matter, but little light, expensive movement and little gas.

None of this is scripted as behaviour — only density, drag, pressure and light.

### Water as a fluid (`World.Waterways.cs`)

Three laws of the group «Water», each with a switch (0 runs the old code bit for bit — checked on the hash of a whole run):
- **Currents (`Currents`).** Water runs between columns by the difference of their surfaces (`Flow`, a share 0.2 of the difference per env step). What runs through a column is its velocity: `CurX = (out east − in from east + in from west − out west) / 2 / max(depth, SwimDepth) / EnvEvery` cells per tick, the same for `CurY`. A body off the bottom (`Lift ≥ 0.5`), in open water, of one cell, is dragged to the water's speed within a few ticks (`WaterFriction`), so the current's displacement accumulates in `Agent.DriftX/DriftY`; each whole cell is a free step to the neighbour at the same absolute height (`SetLift`), only into water it can swim in, never up a wall or onto land, and only where it fits by volume (`Fits`). A body on the bottom holds by its weight. It is the body's own one-cell move in its phase (thread contract: one cell away, the `Drifted` counter through `Interlocked`). There is no wind stress or density-driven circulation in the model, so a flat sea has no current: currents are runoff, rain and evaporation gradients and level differences.
- **Floating ice (`IceFloat`, `IceInsulation` = 0.5).** Ice is lighter than water: it forms on top and the liquid stays under it. Below −1 °C the cold reaches `IceInsulation` blocks into open water per env step at the old rate (2% of that layer), and ice already on top slows freezing by `1/(1 + ice/IceInsulation)` (heat leaves through the ice). A lake no longer freezes through in a winter. The hydraulic head stays `Height + Ice + Water` (floating ice presses on the water with its weight, `Ice` is in water-equivalent blocks), light still loses `WaterDim` per block of ice. A body under the ice is cut off from the air by it as by as much water (`AirBelow`: gas exposure and `AtSurface`). The view draws ice over the water.
- **Water in caves (`CaveWater`).** A run of air under a roof (air voxels on a solid floor up to the rock above) holds water at its bottom: `cave` maps its floor voxel to the depth. Each env step after `Flow`, water passes through every open lateral face by the same law (0.2 of the level difference, from the higher to the lower side, if the water on the higher side reaches the opening): from a neighbouring column's surface water into a cave mouth beside it (the run's side is open above that column's ground), between neighbouring runs whose spans overlap, and back out. No source gives more than it holds, no run takes more than its room. A run filled to its roof does not take more and its level counts as the roof: the air under a roof has nowhere to go but the way the water came (air pockets stay; siphons are not modelled). When rock changes, the water follows: a run opened to the sky gives its water to the column's surface water, a void filled with rock or a run grown too small pushes it into the next run up (at the top onto the surface); a trace below 10⁻⁵ seeps up onto the surface. Cave water is in `WaterTotal` (the liquid part). In a flooded cave a body floats and swims as in any water (no currents there); the cave climate, darkness and gas laws stay as they are. The view draws cave water in the cut (`CaveWaterView`, a copy per env step).
- **Saved** in the version 12 block (`SyncWaterways`): `CurX`, `CurY`, `Drifted`, the cave water map (sorted), every body's drift.
- **Checked** by `--self-test` (`waterways`, also `--self-test-water`): a step in a channel gives a current down it and none across; a swimmer at 0.3 cells/tick is carried 3 cells in 12 ticks at its height and for free, a body on the bottom stays, the bank stops it, the law off — nothing drifts; a 6-block lake at −20 °C for half a year keeps 2.6 blocks of water under 3.4 of ice (old law: frozen through), water conserved, no air under the ice; a tunnel under a hill with its mouth under the sea fills to its roof, a higher chamber behind it only to the tunnel's roof (air pocket), a body floats at the cave water's surface and stays under a full tunnel's roof, save/load keeps it and the continuation, the opened chamber and rock laid into the tunnel move the water without loss, with the sea gone the caves drain except a pool behind a sill, the law off returns the water to the surface. Batch effects: CHANGELOG.

## Cave and depth climate (`World.Cave.cs`)

A law of the environment, not of behaviour: rock above a body stands between it and the sky. What to do about it is decided by the genome. It is switched off by the law `CaveClimate` = 0 — then the code follows the old expressions bit for bit (bench hashes for seeds 1 and 3 up to tick 4000 matched main).
- **Roof** `Roof(cell, z)` — the number of solid blocks above the body's level in its column. `Height` is only an upper bound: voids are not counted. The count is exact, by scanning the `Mat` bytes of the column (they are contiguous), with no cache: a body on the surface (`Z ≥ Height`) costs nothing, an underground one a dozen or two bytes. A swimmer on the surface has roof 0; one in a flooded cave (World.Waterways) has the cave's roof and climate.
- **Shelter** `f = 1 − e^(−roof/CaveDepthK)`: with `CaveDepthK` = 3, under 1 block 28%, under 3 — 63%, under 5 — 81%, under 10 — 96%. A hollow with a single overhang already softens the swings slightly — a gradient from niche to tunnel without a jump.
- **Depth climate** `Tcave = Tmean[cell] + GeoGrad·(Height − 1 − Z) + CaveWarm[cell]`:
  - `Tmean` — a slow mean of the cell's `Temp` (EMA with time constant `TmeanTau` = 12,000 ticks, a year), updated in `UpdateClimate` after diffusion. In a new world (and when loading a file without this block) it starts from the annual climate norm: `TempEq`, with latitude climate and light averaged over 24 season positions and 48 sun positions per day (without clouds and mountain shadows);
  - `GeoGrad` (0.15 °C per level) — geothermal heat by depth below the top of the column;
  - `CaveWarm` — body heat in the cavity (below).
- **Perceived temperature** `LocalTemp = lerp(Temp, Tcave, f)`. It is used by body temperature (`FootTemp`, for a large body the mean over its cells at its level), the `temp` sensor (surroundings), the inspector and the view.
- **Body heat in a cavity.** Expenditures (`Dissipate`) and reaction heat shed by a body go into `heatIn` in full, as before. The fraction `f` of them, if the body is under a roof, is additionally recorded into `caveHeatIn` of its cell; on the climate step the surface gets `heatIn − caveHeatIn`, and the cavity air `CaveWarm += caveHeatIn·HeatToTemp − CaveWarm·TRelax` (like dry land). A cramped cave with a crowd heats up. The energy budget does not change: heat is a sink, the same amount passed through `heatIn` (`HeatMismatch` converges). Heat from decomposition in a body and from death stays on the surface. A cavity is computed per column: neighbouring columns of one cave do not exchange heat.
- **Light, UV, snow, strikes, flares.** Photons and UV (`UvK`) under any roof are 0 anyway (`AgentLight`: `Z < Height` ⇔ roof ≥ 1) — stricter than shelter `f` and consistent with opaque rock. Snow acts on bodies only through surface temperature and light, i.e. also via shelter. An orbital strike's ray irradiates a body with probability `(0.3 + 0.7·k)·(1 − f)`, the solar flare dose is multiplied by `1 − f` (`World.Sky`).
- **Water.** Swimmers are never under a roof; water smooths temperature anyway (`TRelaxWater`). Nothing changed.
- **Parallel contract.** In the creature phase `Roof`/`LocalTemp` only read their own column, `caveHeatIn` is written only into the body's own cell (like `heatIn`). `Tmean` and `CaveWarm` change between ticks.
- **Laws** ("Climate" group): `CaveClimate` (1/0), `GeoGrad` (0–2 °C per level), `CaveDepthK` (0.1–50 blocks), `TmeanTau` (100–10⁶ ticks).
- **Observation.** `World.CaveCensus()` (bench prints it as the `caves:` line, batches write columns): fractions of bodies with roof ≥ 1 and ≥ 3 (`cave1`, `cave3`), the mean over all bodies and the maximum depth below the top of the column for bodies under a roof (`depth_mean`, `depth_max`), voids under a roof in the whole world (`cave_voids`), the fraction of bodies in the polar belts (|latitude| > 60% of the way to the pole) and on the highest fifth of columns, and how many of them are under a roof (`polar_share`, `polar_cave1`, `mount_share`, `mount_cave1`).
- **Verification.** Test `cave climate`: on a flat rock, a niche under 5 and under 1 block, a body inside and a body on the roof, a short year (2 days), two years, no rain, no temperature harm and no altitude cooling. The last day is winter: under 5 blocks it is 4.4 °C warmer, the daily range of `Tb` (around the seasonal trend) is 19% of the surface one; under 1 block — 72%; cavity air is warmed by bodies; the energy budget converges; the `temp` sensor reads the perceived temperature; with the law off, `FootTemp` is again the surface temperature, with the same range as above.
- **Approximations.** A column, not the volume of a cavity: a side entrance to a cave does not "let out" the climate, and a cave under a thin roof with an open side shelters like a closed one. Cavity gas is a fraction of the column's gas (`CaveGasK`, "Resources"), with no cavity volume. Burials in metamorphism still use the surface temperature.

## Depth geochemistry (`World.Geochem.cs`)

A law of the generator, not of behaviour: the distribution of elements in strata is set from the seed, nothing is assigned to bodies. Whether a body needs a deep element is decided only by the chemistry it runs. It is switched off by the law `GeoProfile` = 0 — then strata and volcanoes follow the old code bit for bit (bench hashes for seeds 1 and 3 up to tick 4000 matched main).
- **Depth bias** `DepthBias[e]` ∈ [−1; 1] — a hash of the seed (the world's random streams are untouched). Elements of atmospheric gas (volatile) are surface elements: their bias is ≤ 0. **Generator guarantee:** among the remaining elements, the one in which the world's chemistry holds the most releasable energy (the sum of the energies of exothermic decompositions, divided among the molecule's atoms) gets a bias ≥ `DeepElementMin` (0.7) and becomes the deepest (if another one rolled higher, they are swapped). This is "reduced energy-rich matter lies deep, the surface is what has already given up its energy"; the chemistry does not change, only which element goes deep is chosen.
- **Stratum profile.** A molecule's affinity `prof(s) = Σ_e DepthBias[e]·fraction_e(s)`. A block at depth `d` levels below the original top of the column (`Height0`) chooses a molecule with weight `base(s, rank)·exp(prof(s)·(d − DepthMid)/DepthScale)`: `base` is the earlier strength-based choice (pressure, domains, seam) in the form of a soft window ±2 species wide over the list sorted by bond; the exponential is the profile. Above `DepthMid` (12 levels) surface elements win, below it deep ones, `DepthScale` = 4 levels per e-fold shift at bias 1. The choice uses a coherent 3-D field: neighbouring blocks choose alike (strata and lenses, not speckles).
- **Veins.** A second coherent field, thin vertically (bands); where it is above a threshold, molecules of the deep element get an additional weight `e^(VeinGain·fraction)`. The threshold `VeinThreshold` (0.72) increases toward the surface by up to `+0.6·(1 − threshold)` (0.89 at zero depth): a vein outcropping at the surface is rare.
- **Volcanoes** bring up the interior: a cone molecule is chosen from the earlier energetic ones (`VentHigh`) and the eight deepest by profile at crust depth (`Crust`), with profile weights. The inflow is still external and accounted: `InteriorInput` and the `vent` flux.
- **Saving.** A world stores what it was created with (`GeoOn`, biases, the four law values) — the version 6 block. `Height0` is derived from the seed. Laws of the "Geochemistry" group are read only when a world is created.
- **Results** (`--geochem`, seeds 1–4, no life): the share of the deep element in rock atoms is 2–16% near the surface, 23–75% at 30 levels, 36–84% at 60; volcanic output 38–100%. A deep element bound in one molecule with a surface element rises together with it (seed 4: 16% near the surface). Test `geochem` (12 seeds): at 60 levels the share is ≥ 2.3× the surface one, in veins ≥ 1.4× the surrounding rock, in volcanic output ≥ 3.6× the surface.
- **Observation.** `World.GeoCensus()` and batch columns: `deep_share` — the share of the deep element in the atoms of living bodies; `mined` — molecules torn out of rock since the start; `mined_depth_mean` — their mean depth below the original top of the column; `mined_deep3` — the share from 3+ levels; `mined_deep_atoms` — the share of the deep element in the atoms of what was mined. Counting is done in `Mine` and `SoakAggregate` per tile (`Ctx.GeoMined`) and merged after the creature phase. Bench prints the `geochem:` line; `--geochem` — a chemistry table, rock composition by depth, what bodies hold and what their proteins are doing; `--invade` — spawning an example ("Mole") or a creature-designer file (`--invade design.json`) and following its lineage.
- **View.** Map M "deep element": from above — the share in the top block, in cross-section (C) — by level (slate → ochre → gold), veins lighter.
- **Approximations.** The profile sets only the initial strata and volcanic output: metamorphism, sediments and weathering move matter as before, without their own "geochemical" sorting. Stratum depth is measured from the original top of the column, not from the current relief.

## Resources: stocks, fluxes, locality (`World.Resources.cs`)

Laws of the environment, not of behaviour. Both switches are neutral by default: `GasDiffK` = 1, `CaveGasK` = 0 — the world runs bit for bit as before (bench hashes matched main).
- **What "gas" is.** `Chem.Gas` is a single molecular species (the lightest and weakest; in seeds 1–4 it is an excited form, e.g. `D1*`). Only it diffuses; its ground state (`D1`) is ordinary loose matter that lies where it fell. Atmospheric gas is a primary stock that nobody replenishes: on the ground it relaxes to the ground state (`CellChem`, `SplitExo`), rain adsorbs it into the top block (`EnvChem`), only bodies (excretion, death) and orbital strikes can return it to the air.
- **Diffusion** (`World.Diffuse`): the flux along an edge is `(c_j − c_i)·Diff[Gas]·GasDiffK·w` in `Qty`, truncated toward zero symmetrically, so what one cell gave the other received (test `resources`, atoms exactly). The mean squared path of a patch over `t` ticks is `4·Diff·GasDiffK·t` (verified: ×0.30 at 0.3). At 1 — ~26 cells per day, ~85 per year; at 0.1 — 8 and 26. The coefficient is the same over water and over land.
- **Gas under a roof** (`CaveGasK` > 0): a body under `roof` solid blocks (as computed by `World.Cave.Roof`) has access to a fraction `e^(−roof/CaveGasK)` of its column's gas (`Loose`, `CaveGas`); uptake under a roof first takes gas from the cavity floor (burial), the rest from the column's air (`ChangeLooseAt`). A simple model: the cavity has no pool of its own, the cave and the surface above it drink from the same stock; a gulp is no larger than the accessible fraction, but with many gulps a cavity can drain the column (which is replenished only by diffusion). Without the law there is no atmospheric gas under a roof at all — only what lies on the cavity floor.
- **Excretions** (`expel`) land on the neighbouring accessible floor (`WalkLevel`), gas as well, and are visible to neighbours in `smell`/`sense` (`LooseAmount`). Limitation: `smell` toward a neighbouring column one step higher than the body does not see what lies on it (`Loose` looks at the body's level); `look` does not see matter at all (only bodies and relief).
- **Food-web probe** (`World.FoodProbe`, `src/Sim/World.FoodWeb.cs`, bench `--food-chain`): per lineage (or exact genome) the molecules bodies gain from the environment (`Intake`, `Mine`, `SoakAggregate`) and from other bodies (`attack`, `take`), and kills, with Interlocked counters from the creature phase. Matter-based trophic level, like a tracer isotope: TL = 1 + Σ f·TL of the sources (Levine 1980), the environment counting 0; predation inside a lineage is reported apart and left out of the levels; the chain length is the longest simple path along edges that bring ≥ 5% of the eater's molecules. Approximations: energy passed by `share` and links is not counted, eating remains on the floor counts as environment, a loop of mutual feeding without environment in the window hits the cap 10. Counting only: the state hash with the probe is the same.
- **Ancestor tournament** (bench `--tournament`, `tools/bench/Tournament.cs`): the most common genomes of earlier snapshots or saves against those at the end, planted in equal numbers into a copy of the final world (residents killed unless `--residents`), the share of moderns' descendants after `--arena-ticks`. The main world is not touched.
- **Resource probe** (`World.ResProbe`, bench `--resources`): per 32×32 region (8×5) and per molecular species, records the change in atmospheric gas and loose food by tick phase (diffusion, environment, bodies, other — differences of sums between marks in `Step`), gross uptake (`Intake`), excretion, adsorption by rain, transport by diffusion. Counting only: the world hash with the probe is the same (tested). With the probe a tick costs 5 extra passes over the field (~1.3 million additions).
- **Census** `World.ResCensus()` (batch columns and bench line `resources:`): `gas_share` — the share of body atoms in the gas formula (both forms); `region_diets` — e^H of diet (`World.Diet`) within a region with ≥ 5 bodies, averaged over bodies; `diet_beta` — H(planet) − mean H(region), nats: how much regions differ in diet; `region_pop_cv` — the spread of body counts across regions; `gas_air`, `food_loose` — gas and loose matter per cell; `gas_cv`, `food_cv` — the spread of these stocks across regions (patchiness).
- **View.** Map M "food and gas stock": loose matter on the ground — green → yellow, atmospheric gas — blue, black — eaten out.
- **Saving.** No new state: the laws are saved together with all laws; old files do not have them — neutral values are loaded. The probe is not saved.
- **Approximations.** Cavity gas is a fraction of the column's gas, not a cavity volume with openings; an entrance open on the side is not accounted for. Gas over water diffuses as over land. Loose matter other than gas does not move (only creep and compaction of sediment).

## Leaching: a sink for loose matter (`World.Leach.cs`)

A law of the environment (water and mass), not of behaviour. Off by default (`LeachK` = 0: the world runs bit for bit as before).

**Why.** With body wear on, matter that leaves a body lands at its feet and is taken back in; the dead and the primordial litter lie on the ground for years. The loose stock is a chemostat with an inflow (weathering of top blocks, deaths, excretion, vents) and no outflow, so nothing can deplete.

**Law.** Once every `MetamorphEvery` ticks, of every loose species `s` lying on the surface of a column (`World.C`) the share `LeachK · MetamorphEvery · wet · Diff[s] / max Diff` soaks into the ground:
- `wet = min(1, Water + Rain)` of the column: standing water of a block or more (saturated ground) or rain at full strength percolate fully, dry ground under a clear sky not at all. Deserts keep their litter, wet lowlands lose it.
- `Diff[s] / max Diff` is mobility from mass (`Chemistry.Diff` = 0.13/√mass, the same law that moves the gas), relative to the lightest molecule of the chemistry: light molecules are carried away first. No species is singled out — the air's gas pool is leached like the rest (it is the lightest, so wet ground draws it in fastest); rain adsorption (`EnvChem`) is unchanged.
- The matter moves (`Qty`, exact) into the burial of the block under the top block, where no body on the surface reaches it. If that level is a void, it drips through onto the cavity floor (reachable from the cave). It comes back only by the ordinary paths: when the top block goes (mined through, dug, weathered, crushed), `RemoveVoxel` moves its burials down and the burial below becomes the floor. Burials keep reacting under pressure as before.
- Energy: bond energy moves between two reservoirs (loose → burial) — no new flow. Main thread, environment phase: no agent-tile contract involved.
- Also routed through the registry (defaults unchanged, bit for bit): `LooseDecayK` = 0.0005 (decay of exothermic loose molecules on the ground, formerly a literal) and `WeatherK` = 0.0002 (weathering of the top block, formerly a literal).

**Observation.** `World.Leached` (molecules soaked since the world was made or loaded; not saved), batch column `leached`, `ResProbe.Leach` per region; `--resources` prints food uptake versus leaching and the days a region's loose food would last under uptake alone. Test `leach` (`--self-test`, separately `--self-test-leach`).

**Batches** (CHANGELOG (22)): leaching depletes and patches the loose food (stock ×0.1–0.4, `food_cv` ×3–4), but population, uptake, diets per region, mining depth and the deep element do not change. The diagnosis: in these worlds bodies are limited by energy, not matter (most division attempts fail for lack of energy; at 6000 ticks abiogenesis supplies more bodies than births). See the CHANGELOG for numbers.

**Approximations.** The percolation depth is one block (below the top block), not a soil profile; burials have no volume; there is no dissolved pool in water (water does not carry the matter sideways); frozen ground is not distinguished from wet ground by temperature (only standing liquid water and rain count). Cave floors and the floor burial of the top block are not leached.

## Sky (`World.Sky.cs`)

Laws of the environment, not of behaviour: the sun, the atmosphere, the moon and flares. What to do with them is decided by the genome. Each part has its own switch (`Insolation`, `Transparency`, `ShadeK` = 0 (and `Canopy` 0 or 1 for its variant), `Eclipses`, `Flares`); with all of them off and the old `PhotonK` = 0.06, the code follows the old expressions bit for bit (bench hashes for seeds 1 and 3 at ticks 1000–4000 matched main).

**Insolation (1.1).** Solar power `l = max(0, sin h)^InsolExp` — the cosine law (`InsolExp` = 1): noon at the equator 1, 60° at the equinox 0.5, polar winter 0. Soft terminator: between `TwilightLo` = −0.04 and `TwilightHi` = 0.05 of the sine of elevation, light rises smoothly to `TwilightHi^k`. The old curve `Smooth(−0.04; 0.3)` saturated already at an elevation of ~17°, so the equator and mid-latitudes barely differed in light.
- **Day length** follows from the same formula (`DayShare`: cos H₀ = −tan φ·tan δ). The `--sun` table (tilt 0.33 rad = 18.9°): equator 50% year-round; 45° — 61% in summer, 39% in winter; 60° — 70/30%; 67° — 80/20%; 75° and 82° — 100% in summer, 0% in winter; at the equinox 50% everywhere (51–59% with twilight).
- **Latitude climate** `climRow` — from daily integrated insolation rather than noon elevation: `TPole + (TEquator − TPole)·q^ClimPow`, where `q` is the daily mean power, blended with the annual mean of that latitude (`ClimInertia` = 0.5: thermal inertia of land and ocean), relative to the equator at the equinox; `ClimPow` = 1.35. Without inertia the polar day (daily total at 80° in summer ≈ the equatorial one) would give +29 °C at the pole. Annual means are almost unchanged (equator 27.4 → 27.4 °C, 41° 14.4 → 14.8, 62° 1.2 → 2.6), but the range at the poles is different: at 80° winter −16 → −11.8, summer +1.3 → +10.4 — a warm polar day and a cold night. The climate norm for cave climate (`Tmean` in a new world) is computed by the same law.
- **Photons.** `PhotonK` was recalibrated from 0.06 to 0.13 so that the planet's annual photon input stays the same (together with transparency, which transmits 0.82 on average): a year of a lifeless world, seeds 1–4 — −1.8%, +5.2%, +1.5%, +1.9% relative to before (`--sun`). Test `sun` checks the same by formula: ±10%. Light is redistributed: the noon equator and clear deserts are brighter, morning, evening and high latitudes darker.
- **View and HUD.** Map M "solar power" (2) shows `Sun` — light at the surface, "day length" — polar night black, polar day bright. The HUD top line under the cursor: latitude, day length in hours of the nominal day, noon solar elevation, current power (and at the bottom), transparency.

**Atmospheric transparency (1.2).** The field `Transp[i]` ∈ [`TranspMin` = 0.3; 1]: a latitude profile of wet and dry belts (the same `Wet(v)` as for clouds: hazy near the equator and around 60°, clear around 30°), plus coherent noise — three octaves of `Noise.Value3` with patch size `TranspScale` = 0.25 of the circumference, drifting east by `TranspDrift` = 1 cell per day and slowly changing shape — with amplitude `TranspNoise` = 0.8, plus `TranspAlt` = 0.01 per level above the planet's mean land height. The noise is centred on its planetary mean: otherwise a few large patches made one seed's sky clearer overall than another's (mean transparency across seeds was 0.75–0.89, now 0.820–0.825).
- Recomputed every `LightEvery·8` ticks (and when the law is switched on) from the seed, the tick and heights; the field is stored in the save (it depends on heights at the time of computation).
- **Water.** A clear sky evaporates more and rains less: evaporation × `1 + TranspHydro·(T/T̄ − 1)`, the cell's weight in rain distribution × `1 − TranspHydro·(T/T̄ − 1)` (`TranspHydro` = 0.5). Water is conserved: rain distributes the same moisture by weights (`RainSum`).
- View: map M "transparency"; in the HUD — under the cursor.

**Shading (1.3).** In a cell, light is taken first by bodies higher up (by `Z + Lift`), and at the same level by larger ones. One law for both variants: a body is a layer of transmission `e^(−ShadeK·coverage)`, coverage `= (volume per cell / VoxelSpace)^(2/3)` — the share of the cell's cross-section its own matter takes (from composition: volume is mass over packing); `ShadeK` = 1.5. There is no leaf type and no optical table: molecules have no optical property beyond being excitable, so the layer is geometric (Beer–Lambert by height, the Monsi–Saeki canopy). The switch `Canopy` chooses how the light is shared:
- **Variant A (`Canopy` 0, default).** The cell keeps one photon pool `Photon[cell]` (accrued every `LightEvery`, up to `PhotonCap`). A body catches a photon with probability `e^(−ShadeK·Σ coverage of bodies above it)`; a miss does not spend the photon — it stays for the others. The input does not change, the queue does. A large body covering a cell (`Big`) also shades. In the creature phase only one's own cell's list is read and only one's own `Photon` is written.
- **Variant B (`Canopy` 1, the canopy).** Between ticks, right after the light update, the photons falling on each cell this update, `Sun·PhotonK·LightEvery`, go down through the bodies standing in its light (on the top of the column; the big body covering it too), sorted highest first, on one level the bigger, then the older id. Each body stops `1 − e^(−ShadeK·coverage)` of what reaches it into its own store `Agent.LightQuota` (up to `PhotonCap` per cell it covers; the excess is lost); the water between two layers swallows `e^(−WaterDim·blocks)`; what passes everyone reaches the ground and is lost (`Photon[cell]` then holds that, for panels). `photo` spends only the body's own store (water and the bodies above have had their share), so the creature phase reads and writes nothing of the cell. The pass is parallel by row: a body is written only from its own cell; what a big body stops in the other cells of its footprint is added after the pass in `Agents` order. `photons` (`light.2`) reads the body's store. With `ShadeK` = 0 bodies stop nothing and the shared pool of A is used. Under B a lone body takes only its own cross-section of the cell's light (a body of mass ~10 — 7.9%), where under A it takes all of it.
- Photons are not energy in the ledger: under both variants only the chemical gain of an excitation is booked (`photo`); B's conservation of photons is checked by the test cell by cell.
- Tests: A — on one floor a small body under a large one (shade 0.55) caught 1002 photons versus 1800 without shading, the large one 1800 in both cases; a swimmer near the surface shades one lying on the bottom. B — in a cell of three bodies stopped + ground = inflow to 10⁻⁶; the small one under two gets exactly what passes them (3.3% against 7.9% alone or on top); a swimmer stops before the bottom dweller and the water between takes its share; a big body gets its share in all 5 cells of its footprint; stores stop at `PhotonCap`; `photo` spends the store, not the cell; a canopy world (600 ticks) closes atoms and energy and saves and loads with its stores.
- **Why A stays the default** (CHANGELOG 2026-10-08 (8)): with `ShadeK` 1.5 B takes most light away from small bodies — light eaters 13.5% → 0.2% of bodies, population at 6000 ticks 342 → 127 (16 seeds × 2); with `ShadeK` 15 (a small body stops ~45%) light eaters 13.5% → 6.6%, the population within noise, the top body of a shared cell earns 3.0× the light income of those below (A: 1.8×), but body size and the cover of light eaters do not grow in 6000 ticks.

**Eclipses (1.4).** The moon: the period between new moons is `MoonPeriod` days (0 — from the seed, 3…9), orbital inclination `MoonTilt` (0 — from the seed, 0.2…0.45 rad), distance `MoonDist` = 6 planetary radii; the orbit's nodes rotate (from the seed), so eclipses come in seasons. The geometry is a pure function of the tick (`EclipseAt`): direction to the sun S (from `SunX`, declination), to the moon M (lagging the sun by synodic phase φ, latitude β = inclination·sin(φ + ν)); the shadow axis through the moon parallel to S meets the sphere at `q + √(1 − |q|²)·S`, `q = MoonDist·(M − (M·S)S)`; at |q| ≥ 1 the shadow misses. The centre is converted to cells, the shadow is a circle of `EclipseR` = 20 cells with light × `EclipseDepth` = 0.02 and penumbra out to 1.3·R (almost no photons arrive there). `NextEclipse` predicts the next one — selection may fix code that "knows the time".
- Chronicle (`EvType.Climate`): start (where the shadow is) and end (the track: from which point to which and how many ticks; the first eclipse is important).
- View: the shadow is visible in lighting and on the power map, a pale ring marks the edge of full shadow; HUD: "ECLIPSE: shadow at (x, y)" or "eclipse in N days". For screenshots: `--lookeclipse` (camera to the shadow), `--looksun` (to the day side).
- Frequency: seeds 1–4 in the first 12,000 ticks — 0, 1, 0, 1 eclipses (the moon orbits in 7–8 days). Test: an eclipse is predicted at tick 21,176 (seed 1), 2.0% of light at the shadow centre, 520 ticks, both events in the chronicle, two worlds give the same result.

**Solar activity and flares (1.5).** They replace orbital strikes as mutagenic pressure; the law `Flares` is **on by default**. Strikes remain a manual tool (X, ⇧X) and a new-world option (off by default); the old bench and self-test constructor, which enabled strikes, now enables them only when flares are off.
- **Activity** `A(t)` ∈ [0; 1] = 0.5 + 0.5·sin(2π·t/cycle + φ) + 0.3·(daily noise − 0.5), cycle `SolarCycle` (0 — from the seed, 30…80 days). Solar brightness × `1 ± SolarLumAmp` (0.02) with activity.
- **Schedule** — also a pure function of seed and tick (hashes, without the world's random stream: enabling flares does not shift any other draw). Each 100-tick slot starts a flare with probability `FlareRate·A·100/DayLen` (`FlareRate` = 0.5 per day at full activity); duration `FlareLen·(1/3…2)` (50–300 ticks at 150), power Pareto-distributed from `FlarePowerMin` = 1 with tail `FlarePareto` = 2 (1% of flares stronger than 10). Envelope: rise over 10% of the duration, then decay. Overlapping flares add up; an episode (from start to zero) is one chronicle event.
- **Body dose per tick** = power × exposure × shield. Exposure `SkyExposure` = light at the cell's surface (`Sun`: solar elevation, mountain shadow, transparency, clouds, ice, snow, moon shadow) × (1 − roof shelter, `World.Cave`), or underwater × e^(−`FlareWaterDim`·depth below the surface), `FlareWaterDim` = 0.7. The night side is 0. Body shield `Shield` = e^(−`ShieldK`·Σ mass·packing of its molecules / comfortable volume `Room`) — a property derived from `Chemistry`, not a list; a heavy shield is paid for through `CostMass`.
- **Effect of the dose** (in `LiveBody`, own body only and the tile's `Rng`): mutations along the same path as UV (`UvK`): `FlareMutK·dose·genome length` (`FlareMutK` = 3·10⁻⁵) is added to the per-tick point-mutation probability; protein wear `WearProtein` with fraction `FlareProtK·dose` (0.002, no more than half); damage `Dissipate(FlareHarmK·dose)` (0.02); heating: `FlareHeatK·dose` (0.01) of external energy goes into `HeatHeld` and raises `Tb` the same way as reaction heat, budget flux `flare`. A death where flare damage exceeds expenditures and climate harm has cause `CauseFlare` ("solar flare"), counter `DeathsFlare`. Biography: "under a solar flare" (first time per episode).
- **There is no warning**: activity changes smoothly, the `uv` sensor (this is `light.1`; with flares off it reads light, as before) returns `100·(A + flare power)·exposure` — information only.
- **Chronicle** (`EvType.Climate`): flare start (activity), end — peak power, duration, how many bodies were irradiated, mutations, deaths; important if ≥ 50 died or peak ≥ 5·`FlarePowerMin`.
- **View**: the lit side glows violet-white during a flare; map M "flare dose" (on the open surface, without shield; without a flare dimly — where activity reaches); HUD: activity, "FLARE p"; body card: how much sky reaches it, how much its shield transmits, current dose.
- **Test** `sun`: under 10 blocks the dose is 3.3% of the open one, at the bottom of 6 blocks of water 1.2%, a shield of 60 heavy molecules transmits 2%; the dose heats, wears proteins, takes energy, the `flare` input is recorded, the budget converges; `uv` sensor: 156 in the sun, < 1/20 under a roof. A world with frequent strong flares (`FlareRate` 8, power from 3, 1500 ticks): atoms and energy converge, bodies were irradiated, mutated and died from the flare.

**Observation.** `World.SkyCensus()`: `diet_region` — mean e^H of diet over 32×32 tiles with ≥ 5 bodies (regions), `diet_ratio` — e^H of the whole planet's diet divided by it (1 — regions eat alike), `flare_dose` — mean dose per body in the last tick, `transp_mean`, `flares`, `eclipses`, `deaths_flare`. Bench prints the `sky:` line, batches write columns. `--sun` — a table of day length, latitude climate under the old and new law, the schedule of eclipses and flares for the first 12,000 ticks per seed, and the annual photon input of a lifeless world versus the old law.

**Approximations.** Transparency is a field, not transport of aerosols and moisture; clouds are still separate noise. Shading is a per-photon probability (A) or layers of transmission (B), with no leaf geometry and no side light from neighbouring cells. Under B a body's store accumulates between light updates (up to `PhotonCap`, like the cell pool in A) and goes with the body if it changes cell before spending it; switching B → A leaves the stores in place (A does not read them). The moon's shadow is a circle in map cells, without elongation at the terminator and without the distortion of the cylindrical projection near the poles; at the edge of the disc the shadow is not partial. A flare does not affect loose matter and rock (a strike did), its dose is the same for the whole of a large body (by its main cell). Light, transparency, eclipses and flares do not enter the energy budget as a stock — only actual fluxes (`photo`, `flare`).

## Non-stationary climate and catastrophes (`World.ClimateCycles.cs`)

Laws of the environment, not of behaviour: what to do in an ice age or under ash is decided by the genome. Switch `ClimateCycles` (1 by default); with it off and without player catastrophes no branch of this file executes, and the world follows the old expressions bit for bit (bench hashes for seeds 1 and 3 at ticks 1000–4000 matched main; the "off" batch below has the same trajectories).
- **Orbit and sun are pure functions of seed and tick** (no random streams): tilt `Tilt(t) = Tilt ± TiltAmp·sin(2π(t − T0)/TiltPeriod)` (sign from the seed); eccentricity `e(t) = EccAmp·(1 − cos(2π(t − T0)/EccPeriod))/2`; the perihelion goes around the year in `PrecPeriod`; the sun at each moment × `1 + 2e·cos(year angle − perihelion)` — the hemisphere whose summer coincides with perihelion gets a hotter summer; luminosity drift `1 ± SunDriftAmp·sin(2π(t − T0)/SunDriftPeriod)` multiplies the earlier activity-driven variation (`SolarLumAmp`, "Sky"). Periods of 0 come from the seed: tilt 40…120 days, precession 20…50, eccentricity 100…300, drift 200…600. `T0` is the tick from which cycles are counted: 0 in a new world, the load tick for a file without the cycles block, and the tick at which the law was switched on during a run. At `T0` all cycles are in a neutral phase (tilt = `Tilt`, circular orbit, luminosity 1): the world does not start with an ice age.
- **Light and climate.** Light (`Sun`, `Light`, photons) × the solar multiplier × e^(−ash). Latitude climate (`climRow`, and with it `TempEq` and everything downstream) shifts by `ClimSens·(sun·ash transmission in the row − 1)` (25 °C per unit: −3% light — −0.75 °C) and by ice-age cooling (below). Solar declination and eclipses use tilt `Tilt(t)`; the annual mean insolation for latitude climate (`yearInsol`) is recomputed when the tilt has moved by 0.002 rad.
- **Ice ages.** The index is daily insolation at 65° of a hemisphere at its summer solstice × the orbital multiplier there × drift, relative to the norm (tilt `Tilt`, circular orbit): ±`TiltAmp` (0.05 rad) gives ±11%, eccentricity 0.04 — up to ±8%. Hemispheric glaciation `G` (state, saved) tends to 1 when the index is below `IceAgeThreshold` (93%), to 0 above it (smoothly within ±1.5% of the threshold), with time constant `IceAgeTau` (4 days). A latitude row φ is colder by `IceAgeDT·(0.4 + 0.6·|sin φ|)·(0.7·G of its own hemisphere + 0.3·G of the other)` (8 °C at the pole, 3.2 °C at the equator). From there the existing water cycle works: where it is colder than zero, rain falls as snow, water in shallows and seas freezes (`Water → Ice` 2% per environment step at T < −1), snow on land holds water evaporated from the sea, and the existing snow feedback (−4 °C in `TempEq`, less light) amplifies the cold. Sea level drops because water goes into ice and snow: it is the same water.
- **Water budget.** `WaterTotal()` = Σ(water + ice + snow) + atmospheric moisture. It is changed only by the hand ("flood", `PourWater`) and by a deluge — both record what was brought in `WaterHand`; precipitation, evaporation, runoff, melting and freezing only move it. Verified: seed 1 world, 30,000 ticks with cycles (ice age from day 20) — 27,146.3 at each of 15 marks; test: 4 days of an induced ice age — relative change −4·10⁻⁸ (float rounding), after a deluge the difference equals `WaterHand`.
- **Volcanic winters.** Day d carries a mega-eruption if hash(seed, d) < `MegaEruptionRate` (0.01 per day); the tick within the day is also a hash. The location is one of the active volcanoes (chosen by hash), without volcanoes — the lowest of 64 cells by hash. `Erupt` puts `MegaEruptionBlocks` (60) blocks of interior matter in a circle around the vent, like a volcanic cone (`VentMolecule`, `InteriorInput`, input `vent`, `DisplaceOccupants`, `Repose`), and ejects ash: a Gaussian patch (σ 6 cells) with total optical depth `MegaAsh·N` (0.4 — the mean depth over the planet once it has spread). The field `Veil` (saved) is mixed along latitude once per environment step (time constant 0.3 days), spreads toward the poles by diffusion of row means (reaching the poles in about `AshSpreadDays`, 4 days) and settles out with time constant `AshTau` (10 days). Ash is purely optical: its matter is not modelled (the ejection of blocks is fully accounted).
- **Chronicle** (`EvType.Climate`, important): start of an ice age (which hemisphere, summer index at 65°, tilt, eccentricity), end (duration, maximum snow and ice cover, water in snow and ice, how far sea level dropped — the decrease in liquid water divided by the sea area at the start), mega-eruption (location, blocks, ash), start of a volcanic winter (mean fraction of light through ash < 90%) and its end (> 97%: duration, minimum light, mean temperature before and at the worst moment). An ice age starts at `max(G) ≥ 0.5` and ends at `< 0.25`.
- **HUD** — an "epoch" line under the sky line: glacial or interglacial (hemispheric glaciation), volcanic winter (light through ash), droughts, tilt with direction and cycle phase, eccentricity and where the perihelion is, solar drift, summer at 65° per hemisphere and the threshold. Map M "transparency" darkens under ash, the surface under ash turns grey. The "New world" window — a "Climate cycles" checkbox.

**Player catastrophes** (`World.Catastrophe(Catastrophe, out error)`, between ticks; in the game — window F11 with confirmation, command `SimRunner.Catastrophe`; in bench — `--catastrophe-at "TICK:spec"`). Each one is a mechanism the world already has, with accounting of matter, water and energy; they work even with the `ClimateCycles` law off:

| catastrophe | spec | mechanism | accounting |
|---|---|---|---|
| ice age for N days | `iceage days=N` | glaciation of both hemispheres tends to 1 until the end of the term, then follows the law again | same water |
| deluge | `flood k=K` | water of seas and lakes (cells ≥ `SwimDepth`) + K blocks, then the usual runoff | `WaterHand` |
| volcanic winter | `volcano x= y= blocks= ash=` | `Erupt` at a location (x, y ≥ 0) or at a volcano | `InteriorInput`, input `vent` |
| strong flare | `flare power= days=` | the flare is added to the `FlareAt` schedule (same envelope); dose → mutations, proteins, damage, heating; works even with the `Flares` law off | input `flare` |
| drought | `drought x= y= r= days= dt=` | anticyclone: clouds × (1 − w) within radius r (smoothly to 1.5r), no rain there — moisture falls elsewhere, `TempEq` + dt·w | same water |
| poisoning | `poison x= y= r= n= [s5=0.6 …]` | the hand scatters, as loose matter, n molecules at the centre (less toward the edge) of the world's most reactive species by the reactive-damage law (`Chem.MostReactive`), or of a matter-library substance (number fractions by species, `sK=f`); whether it harms is the reactive-damage law's business (World.React) | `HandInput`, input `hand` |

Each is recorded in the chronicle (`Player`, important) and in the law log as the line `catastrophe <spec>` (the value is the kind number): trajectory = seed + log. A test replays the log on a fresh world and gets the same world.

- **Saving** — the version 10 block ("Saving", item 17). No new energy fluxes.
- **Observation.** `World.ClimCensus()` (batch columns): `tilt_deg`, `ecc`, `sun_out`, `summer65_n`/`summer65_s`, `glaci_n`/`glaci_s`, `veil_trans`, `ice_ages`, `volc_winters`, `mega_eruptions`, `water_liquid`/`water_frozen` (fractions of all water), `sea_cells`, `snow_cover`. Bench prints the `climate cycles:` line; `--climate [--seeds 1-8] [--days 40]` — a per-seed schedule without a run: periods, days below the ice-age threshold, mega-eruption days. Batches write, next to the run's CSV, `*.events.csv` — climate, player and evolution-pace events with ticks.
- **Parallel contract.** Everything happens between ticks on the world thread (parallel row passes write only their own rows); in the creature phase, as before, only `Sun`/`Light`/`Temp` are read.
- **Approximations.** Glaciation is a shift of latitude climate, not an ice sheet: ice does not flow, does not load the crust and does not change relief, water accumulates on land as snow; "sea level" is water depth in cells. Ash is an optical depth without matter and without deposition onto the ground; mixing along latitude is fast, toward the poles it is diffusion of means. The ice-age index is the insolation of the solstice day, not the caloric half-year. A drought is an anomaly specified by the player, without its own atmospheric dynamics. Poisoning uses a single toxic molecule — the first in the chemistry's list.

## Wear of body matter (`World.Wear.cs`)

Two laws of chemistry, not behavioural rules; each has its own switch (`PhotoDamage`, `WearK`; 0 — off, the world runs bit for bit as before: bench hashes for seeds 1 and 3 matched main). Registry group "Wear".

**Why.** Without them a body is an eternal battery: light excites a molecule the body already holds (`D1 → D1*`), decomposition-relaxation (`D1* → D1`) returns the energy, no matter is consumed (CHANGELOG (13): 51–91% of living bodies took up not a single molecule during their lives). Measurement before the laws (seeds 1–4, 6000 ticks): 90–98% of molecules in bodies are monatomic (ground and excited), whose only allowed decomposition is relaxation. Therefore "photolysis only via `SplitA`/`SplitB`" in the narrow sense would not affect monatomic batteries at all.

**What a body is here.** The molecules it holds (`Agent.Inv`) and their cohesion (`Chemistry.Bond`); there is no separate membrane in the model (uptake takes from the floor, excretion puts onto the floor). A molecule that has lost its bond with the body ceases to be part of it (`LoseMolecule`):
- if it has an allowed energy-releasing decomposition (`SplitA ≥ 0` and `SplitEnergy ≥ 0`: decomposition of a compound molecule into its constituents, relaxation of an excited monatomic one), it leaves this way: the energy as heat into the body's cell (`heatIn` and the budget flux `body decay`, as with the earlier spontaneous decomposition), the products onto the cell's floor (`ChangeLoose`: loose matter on the surface or the cavity floor; gas into the column's air), as with frostbite;
- otherwise (a ground-state monatomic molecule has no decomposition; an endothermic decomposition would create energy) it leaves whole, with its bond energy — a transfer between budget reservoirs, no flux.

Atoms are moved as whole molecules: one molecule out of the body, its products onto the floor (test `photodamage`, `ElementBudget` before and after matches exactly).

**A. Photodamage (photolysis).** A photon caught by a body (`World.Photo`) leaves excitation energy `x = E[excited] − E[ground]` in one molecule. With probability `PhotoDamage · e^(−PhotoHold · retention / x)` this energy breaks the molecule's bond instead of being stored. Retention = the excited molecule's own cohesion (`Bond`) + `PhotoCage` × the body matrix: the mean cohesion of what the body holds × fill `min(1, molecules / Room)` (a "cage" of strong matter holds fragments in). The chance is computed once per `photo` command (before the loop over photons), randomness is one number per caught photon from the tile's `Rng`, only with the law on. Protection is not written as a rule; it follows from existing mechanics: composition (strongly bound photoactive molecules, a dense strong matrix — which costs mass upkeep), re-uptake of products (they lie at the body's feet, `intake` costs energy) and their reassembly by a protein (`bind`). The photon's energy is already recorded in the `photo` input; on breakage it leaves as heat (`body decay`) or together with the molecule onto the floor.

**B. Wear (thermal and chemical ageing).** Each molecule of a body loses its bond per tick with probability `WearK · TempFactor(Tb) · e^(−WearHold · Bond)`: weakly bound matter leaves first, heat accelerates it (like any reaction; the cold of a cave slows it). The number of departures per tick is the integer part of the expectation plus one with probability equal to the fractional part; which molecule — by weights `Inv·e^(−WearHold·Bond)`. The earlier decomposition `DecayK` (unstable molecules decompose inside the body, products remain) is untouched and works as before.

**Parameters:** `PhotoDamage` = 0 (0…1), `PhotoHold` = 10, `PhotoCage` = 1; `WearK` = 0 (0…0.01), `WearHold` = 3. Orders of magnitude: the excited monatomic "batteries" of seeds 1–4 (`Bond` 0.07–0.5, `x` 4–8) break with a fraction of 0.5–0.85 of `PhotoDamage`; hard molecules (`Bond` ≥ 0.85) tens of times less often.

**Batch** (CHANGELOG (15), 8 seeds × 2, 36,000 ticks). Before the booms, bodies with photodamage take up 2–3 times more often; by day 30 the worlds are smaller (at `PhotoDamage` 1 — 13 times smaller, no booms), with less code, fewer proteins and niches, more coexisting lineages and more "miners"; under a roof, mining depth, the deep element, regional diet — not higher. Wear at 2·10⁻⁴ is noise. There is no interaction with gas locality (`GasDiffK` 0.3, `CaveGasK` 3). Therefore both laws are off by default; for experiments — `PhotoDamage` 0.3, `WearK` 2·10⁻⁴.

**Parallel contract.** Both laws run in the creature phase on the body itself: they write only its own cell (loose matter, `heatIn`) and the fluxes of its own tile; counters `LostPhoto`, `LostWear` use `Interlocked`.

**Observation.** `World.WearCensus()` (batch columns and the bench `matter:` line): `took_in` — the fraction of living bodies that took up at least one whole molecule during their life (`intake`); `intake_rate` — whole uptakes per 1000 ticks of life (Σ uptakes / Σ ages); `matter_rate` — the same together with mined (`mine`, `soak`) and stolen; `body_mols` — molecules per body; `lost_photo`, `lost_wear` — molecules that left bodies under each law per 1000 body-ticks since the last census; `deaths_broken` — bodies that fell apart (below `MinBody`) since the world began. Counters `LostPhoto`/`LostWear` are observation only and are not part of the save (like the profile): they are counted afresh after loading.

**Saving.** No new world state: the laws are saved together with all laws; old files do not have them — they load switched off.

**Approximations.** Fractional uptake (`Pend`) and protein substrate do not wear (proteins decay anyway, `EnzDecay`). Fragments land on the floor of the body's cell in full, without dispersing; gas goes into the column's air (under a roof — onto the cavity floor). Photodamage does not depend on flare dose: a flare acts separately (`Flare`). The body matrix is a mean cohesion without spatial structure.

## Reactive damage (`World.React.cs`)

One law for every species; there is no poison class (until 2026-10-08 some excited molecules were marked `Toxic` and only they seeped into bodies and wore proteins). A molecule a body is exposed to — one it holds, or one lying loose in a cell it stands on — reacts with the body's protein substrate with chance

- `ReactK · Reactivity[s] · TempFactor(Tb)` per held molecule per tick,
- `ReactContact` times that per lying molecule,

where `Reactivity[s] = AffinityPerAtom[s] × Excitation[s]` (`Chemistry`): mean affinity of its atoms × the excitation energy it carries, `E[s] − E[ground]` (0 for every ground state). The reaction spends that excitation: the molecule drops to its ground state (same atoms; on the floor for a lying one), the energy goes to the cell as heat (output `body decay`), and one random protein of the body loses `ReactWear` of its activity — its substrate returns to the membrane pool (`WearProtein`), so no atom is created or lost. A body without proteins has nothing to hit. "Poison" is whatever this law makes harmful: excited, high-affinity matter; `World.Harmful(ch, s)` is only a label (one held molecule wears proteins at least as fast as `EnzDecay` at 15 °C) used by the HUD and editors.

- **Exposure** only through what a body already touches: what it holds (taken in by the existing uptake paths) and what lies on its floor. Nothing seeps in by itself.
- **Cost.** One held molecule drawn by count stands for all of them (expected events `Σ n_s·p_s`, exact while below one per tick); one excited species drawn at random stands for everything lying in one of its cells (× their number). O(1) per body; only bodies with proteins draw random numbers.
- **Parameters** (group Wear): `ReactK` = 3·10⁻⁴ (0 off), `ReactContact` = 0.2, `ReactWear` = 0.5. At the defaults the most reactive excited species (reactivity ~10–16) react ~0.003–0.005 per held molecule a tick at 15 °C; a typical excited molecule (~5) ~0.0015.
- **Vents.** `Vent.Toxic` stays in the save format but no law reads it (nor `High`, `Mid`); it is now an excited species drawn at random (the draw keeps the random stream of worlds that had a toxin). `Chemistry.Low` (test fixtures, `VentMid`) is now "low-energy ground states" (`E ≤ 5`, no excitation) instead of "not poison".
- **Poisoning** (F11) sprays the world's most reactive species by the law (`Chem.MostReactive`), or a library substance (`poison … s5=0.6 s13=0.4`).
- **Saving.** No new state; the laws travel with the save (older files load with the defaults). Counters `ReactHeld`/`ReactLying` are observation only.
- **Approximations.** Every protein is equally vulnerable (no protein-specific chemistry); the excitation is the only energy spent (a ground state never reacts, however high its affinity); lying exposure saturates at one event a tick per body.

## Player hand (brush)

`World.Hand.cs`. The brush acts between ticks, not in the creature phase. Its circle has weight `1 − (d/r)²`: the effect weakens from the centre to the edge, so a stroke piles up a hill, not a slab.
- **Pour** (`Pour`). Per stroke, at the centre, a quarter of a full block of molecules of the chosen species; gas cannot be poured. The matter is laid down like any deposit via `Deposit`: tops up the top block, starts a new one, slides off when too steep (`Repose`) and pushes aside those standing there. The heap is fresh and disordered (order 12), hence loose: easily eaten and easily slides.
- **Flood** (`PourWater`). Water is added to the depth, then flows, evaporates and falls as rain like ordinary water.
- **Dig** (`DigOut`). The top block of a column is removed with a chance that falls toward the edge of the circle. Its molecules leave the world with the hand. Loose matter on the surface remains, matter buried in the block falls to the floor below. Those standing there fall through and are caught by `SettleAgent`. The interior boundary cannot be dug.
- **Kill** (`KillIn`). All bodies in the circle die, including large ones touching it with their cells. Remains stay in place. The cause of death is `CauseHand`, counter `DeathsHand`.

- **Pour a recipe** (`PourMatter`). A recipe of the matter library (below), resolved to this world's species: number fractions and a lattice order. As blocks: per dab at the centre a quarter of a voxel's room, the molecule count taken from the room each species takes at that order (`PackedVolume`: an ordered recipe packs more into a block), laid by `Deposit` (tops up the floor block at that block's own order, then new blocks at the recipe's order). Loose: the same molecules scattered on the surface (the gas joins the air). Atoms go to `HandInput`, bond energy to the `hand` input, exactly like `Pour`.

**Choosing the material** (game, `Main`): each stroke of brush 1 takes a new random pourable species (`RandomPourable`, from the world's random numbers, on its thread) unless the material is locked: Z keeps the current one for every stroke, ⇧Z steps to the next species (skipping the gas), I takes the **exact recipe** of the top block under the cursor (its molecules by species and its order; with no block there, the loose matter lying there): it is read between ticks, brush 1 then pours exactly that, and the sample waits in the matter library (J) to be saved. Z also leaves a library recipe for single species. Choosing reads the world only.

**Chronicle of the hand.** A stroke (button down to button up) is one `Player` entry, written when it ends (`BeginStroke` with the first dab, `EndStroke` via `SimRunner.EndStroke`): what was poured and how many molecules, how much water, how many bodies killed, how many blocks dug, and where. Not important (does not crowd the main list); the stroke accumulator is not saved — a stroke cut by a save loses only its summary line.

Matter brought in or taken out by the hand is accounted in `HandInput` per element, like the inflow from the interior in `InteriorInput`. The atom budget (`--audit`, test `hand`) subtracts both. The hand is the only way to bring matter into the world or remove it from outside.

## Regions: copy and paste a piece of the world (`World.Region.cs`, `Region.cs`)

A **region** is a rectangle of columns (any size up to the world) over a range of levels `Z0 ≤ z < Z1` (full height by default), cut out as plain data. It keeps what the save keeps of those cells: every block (`Mat`, `Units`, `Order` — lattice order and so porosity —, `Pressure`), mixture counts per species, burials (fractional matter in `Qty`, order, pressure), and, where the column's surface lies in the range, its loose matter, `LooseVolume`, water, ice and snow. Optionally the bodies standing in it, each as the save's own body record (`SyncAgent`: genome, protection, proteins, stack, memory, counters), tagged with the save format version, plus lineage, generation and design name. A chemistry signature travels with it: the seed and every species' formula, mass, bond energy, bond strength, packing, volume, colour and names.

- **Copy** (`World.CopyRegion(x, y, w, h, z0, z1, bodies)`): x wraps, rows must lie in the world.
- **Paste** (`World.PasteRegion(region, x, y, PasteOptions)`): rotation in quarter turns (columns permuted, body velocities turned), mode `Replace` (the copied levels of every target column are taken out, then the region is laid in) or `AboveGround` (nothing is taken out: region blocks fill only the air above the target ground; loose matter and water are added only where the region's surface lies at or above it), `Bodies` (off = only matter: bodies there stay, the region's are not brought; on with `Replace`, bodies in the pasted levels are taken away), level shift `Dz`. Between ticks only (`SimRunner.Do`), never in the creature phase.
- **Nothing for free.** A paste is the hand's work. Everything taken out (blocks, burials, the surface's loose matter, water, ice, snow, whole bodies) leaves the world booked as taken by the hand, everything brought is booked as brought: atoms in `HandInput`, bond energy and the bodies' free energy (`Energy` + `HeatHeld`) in the ledger's `hand` input (free energy also in `HandEnergy`), water in `WaterHand`. The sums are taken exactly (whole molecules and `Qty` units per species) and booked once. A target column already equal to the region's is left untouched. Geometry changes go through `TerrainChanged`, the strength caches and the support solver's dirty columns: a pasted overhang may cave in at the next structure pass. A remaining body whose voxel is now rock is lifted onto it (then falls by `SettleAgent` like any body); big bodies draw their feet in and spread anew. Pasted bodies get new ids, no links, targets or biography, join the family tree as founders; in the same world they keep their lineage. A paste is a chronicle event (`EvType.Player`, `Loc.Both`).
- **Another chemistry.** With the same chemistry (`RegionChemistry.SameAs`) nothing is mapped. Otherwise each species becomes this world's nearest by ground/excited state, mass, bond energy, bond strength, packing and size; the region's gas becomes the world's gas. No molecule is invented and molecule counts are kept; a block that no longer fits its voxel by volume puts the excess loose on its column. Atoms and energy are booked in this world's chemistry. Bodies: inventory, partial uptake and protein substrate are mapped the same way (mass and volume follow), as are protein targets; genomes stay as they are (a warning says their genes now name other species); each source lineage becomes a new lineage. The mapping is listed in `RegionMapping.Lines`.
- **Sizes.** A region larger than the world, or whose levels lie entirely outside it, is refused with a reason and nothing changes; rows beyond the poles and levels above the world are clipped with a warning.
- **Files.** Binary `.region`: an uncompressed header (`RegionInfo`: name, size, levels, seed, tick, date, body and block counts) and a Brotli body, little-endian; JSON `.region.json` with the same fields (body state as base64). `Region.Load` reads both. A 64 × 64 full-height region with 349 bodies: 477 KB.
- **Verification** (test `regions`): binary and JSON round trips give the same cells and body records; paste elsewhere and copy back gives the same cells, also turned; after every paste mode the world's atom change equals the hand's booking to 10⁻⁶, energy drift < 10⁻³, water closes; a refused paste leaves the world unchanged; a place overwritten by another region and given its own region back is the original world bit for bit (cells, mixtures, burials, water, pressures) and runs on with the same state hash for 200 ticks; pasting identical cells moves nothing; a paste into another chemistry maps all species, gas to gas, and its budgets close.
- **Approximations.** The solver re-solves pasted columns and their neighbours, so their pressures are those of a fresh pass, not of the original's older passes. Temperature, light, clouds and face work (`Bite`, reset when the face's block changed) are not carried: they are climate and history fields, not matter. Chemistry mapping is a property heuristic (elements differ between seeds, formulas cannot be matched). The map selection does not cross the x seam (pasting does).
## Matter library (`Materials.cs`, `MatterWindow`, key J)
A substance is a **recipe of this world's real molecules**: number fractions by species (`MatterPart`), a lattice order 0…255 (which sets packing and porosity through `PackedVolume`) and a form (blocks, or loose/spray). Name, kind ("poison", "mineral", "organic"…) and colour are the player's labels only; nothing in the engine reads them and no material has code of its own.
- **Card** (`MatterRecipe.Props`, the engine's own formulas): molecules per block (`BlockCapacity`) and per unit of volume, order and porosity (`1 − Σ f·Volume / Σ f·PackedVolume`), mass per molecule and density, cohesion (mean bond × pairwise contact, as `CoreCohesion`), gnaw barrier (as `VoxelBarrier`), what a full block bears (as `CompressionCapacity` at fill 1), reactive harm per held and per lying molecule at 15 °C and in units of `EnzDecay`, bond energy per molecule and per block. The self-test checks the card against a real block made from the same mix (`matter library`).
- **Starters** (`MatterRecipe.Starters`), computed per seed from its chemistry: hardest crystal (strongest bond, order 255), densest compacted (max mass per packed volume, order 255), most reactive mixture (top 3 by `Reactivity`, in its proportion), lightest volatile (the world's gas), energy-rich deposit (max bond energy per volume with some of its ground state, order 60), softest heap (weakest bond, order 0), inert low-energy fill (three ground states of least energy). No species is fixed.
- **Sampling** (read only, between ticks): the top block of a column exactly (counts and order), the loose matter on a surface, the molecules a body holds.
- **Files**: JSON in `user://materials/` with the chemistry signature (`ChemSignature`: seed, per element mass/valence/affinity, per species composition, mass, bond, packing, mean atom affinity, energy, excitation). Same chemistry = every species has the same composition and energy (the seed alone is not trusted). **Another chemistry**: each species of the recipe goes to the nearest species of this world by property distance — composition fractions by element role (elements ranked by atomic mass, the only order two worlds share), |ln mass ratio|, relative bond difference, packing, excited/ground, |ln energy ratio|, atom count — and the window shows the mapping (with distances) and a warning; no molecule is invented. The mapped recipe is a new draft of this world; the file stays as it was.
- **Order and energy.** The engine has no energy for lattice order: pressure orders blocks in `Metamorphose` without an energy term, and crushing disorders them without one. So pouring a highly ordered recipe books its matter and bond energy; its order is player content.
- **Poisoning** (F11) can spray any library substance (`Catastrophe.Mix`); whether it harms is the reactive-damage law's business.
- **View.** Overlay 8 ("matter: lattice order and packing"): the top block in its material colour, brighter the more ordered, darker the more mass per volume (against the world's densest packing); the shader draws facets with gloss and edges that grow with order (`facet_map`, a W×H RG8 texture uploaded at most 4 times a second while the overlay is on), grains when disordered; the cross-section (C) shows every level the same way. The card's picture shows the same cues.
- **Limits.** A recipe is a mix plus one scalar order; it has no spatial structure inside a block. Pouring as blocks tops up the floor block at that block's order, as any deposit does. Sampling a block leaves out the remains buried in its voxel; sampling a body leaves out protein substrate and partial uptake.

## Limits: cost instead of caps

Principle: a body has no "full" and no "not allowed". The more it holds, the more expensive it is to hold and the more expensive to add.
- **Body matter.** There is no molecule cap. "Comfortable" is `InvPerCell` (64) per occupied cell (`Agent.Room`). With fullness `Packing = molecules/Room`, mass upkeep costs `CostMass·mass·(1+Packing²)`, intake costs `CostIntake·(1+Packing²)`. Mining, theft, attack, decomposition and sharing no longer "fail to fit".
- **Energy.** There is no cap. The stock leaks as heat: `HoldK·E·(E/Store)²` per tick, where `Store = 30 + 1.5·mass` is the "comfortable stock". Below it the leak is small, at twice the stock it is 8 times larger; it is shown in the inspector as "spent on holding the stock".
- **Proteins.** There is neither a limit on species (the array grows) nor on the amount of a single protein. Each unit carries its substrate molecule (mass), decays (`EnzDecay`) and costs expression. Reaction turnovers per command are no longer limited to 4 — they are limited by substrate.
- **Colony links** — any number, each costs `CostLink` per tick.
- **Gene insertion and excision** — no more than the genome length, each byte costs. **Attack** — as far as energy allows.
- **Genome.** Length is limited by the price of a byte (upkeep, copying, insertion). `Genome.MaxLen = 4096` is only a memory safeguard, far beyond what a body can afford.

What remained a law of the world or a technical boundary:
- **A body** covers no more than `MaxCells` = 10 cells (footprint geometry, radius 2). Each cell requires mass `GrowMass·k^GrowPow` and costs `CostCell`, so bodies in runs did not exceed 6–7 cells.
- **Light** does not accumulate: a cell holds no more than `PhotonCap` photons; it is a flux, not a store.
- **`look`** sees up to 16 cells. Parallel stepping relies on this radius (see below).
- **VM "hardware":** stack and memory of 16 slots each, 8 nested calls, base cycle costs.
- **World dimensions:** 4 elements, 32 molecular species, a full block is `MatCap` molecules by volume (hundreds to thousands), height 192 (`P.ZMax`). Crust `World.Crust` = 60 under the lowest places plus the relief: the first relief (~28 levels from valleys to mountains) stretched `ReliefScale` times (a world-generation law, default 1; at 4: ~115 levels, highest columns ~175). The default stays 1 until slopes are generated within rock strength: at 4 the mountains slump heavily and the population falls ~3× (CHANGELOG 2026-10-09 (2)). Bodies get over ledges by momentum (above), so steep slopes are climbable at a cost. Altitude climate is measured from the relief's reference height `Crust + 3 + 8·ReliefScale`; `TLapse` (0.6 °C) is per level of the first relief and a world cools `TLapse / ReliefScale` per level (`World.Lapse`), so the valleys-to-peaks span is the same ~13 °C at any scale; hot springs are placed in lowlands up to `Crust + 3 + 6·ReliefScale`. The world keeps the scale it was made with (save version 15; older worlds: 1). The view packs levels into 4 texture bands (1024×7680) to stay within the GPU limit.

## Budgets

### Elements

`ElementBudget()` counts atoms in:

- the molecules of all non-empty blocks;
- the surface environment and gas;
- burials;
- living bodies, `Pend`, protein material.

Call it between completed ticks, when `newborn` has already been joined to `Agents`. The initial state includes the procedural primordial stock. The checked quantity is `budget(t) - InteriorInput(t) - HandInput(t) - budget(0)`. The bottom boundary contains no minable atoms. The volcanic reservoir lies outside the computed crust and has an explicit inflow counter.

The number of molecules need not be conserved: the element vector is conserved. **Fractional pools are exact.** Loose matter and gas (`C`), burials (`Burial.Matter`), partial uptake (`Agent.Pend`) and protein substrate (`Enzyme.Matter`) are stored in `Qty` fixed point (`src/Sim/Qty.cs`): an integer number of 2⁻³² molecules in a `long`. Addition and subtraction are integer, so a transfer — the same quantity taken here and put there — conserves atoms bit for bit, however large the pools are. A fraction from float/double is rounded to the grid once, when converted to `Qty`, symmetrically with respect to sign (`−m` and `+m` are the same quantity). Gas diffusion computes the flux on each edge from the same difference and the same weight on both sides and truncates the fraction toward zero: what one cell gave the other received. `Qty` may be read into double implicitly (exactly), into float only explicitly (`.F`), so that loss of precision is visible in the code. A new path that moves fractional matter must move `Qty`, not a float computed from the pool (otherwise rounding reappears). `ElementBudget` sums in integers (whole molecules and fractional pools in `Qty` units) and converts to double only at the end: the reading is exact to ~10⁻⁷ atom, and any visible drift is real. Reactions and game actions must not create a whole molecule from almost one, delete protein substrate or replace residual energy with new matter.

### Energy (`World.Energy.cs`)

**Stock** — everything that can still do chemical work (`AuditEnergy()`, scans the entire crust — on demand only):
- free energy of bodies `Agent.Energy` (may be negative, see debt below);
- reaction heat still held by the body, `Agent.HeatHeld`;
- bond energy `Chemistry.E[s]` of all molecules: in bodies (`Inv` and fractional `Pend`), in protein substrate, loose on the ground and gas in the air (`C`), in rock blocks and mixtures, in burials (`Buried`).

Heat in cells (`heatIn` → `Temp`) is outside the stock; it is a sink.

**Fluxes** (cumulative, `EnergyFlows()`, names in `World.FlowNames`):
- *inputs*: a photon raised a molecule to an excited state (`photo`); an impact from space excited loose matter (`strike`); a solar flare heated a body (`flare`: external energy goes into heat held by the body, `HeatHeld`, and leaves into cells as shed reaction heat, `shed`; flare damage is an ordinary expenditure `dissipate`); a volcano brought matter from the interior (`vent`); the hand poured (+) or removed (−) matter (`hand`); a custom creature was spawned with matter or energy from outside (`design`: the bond energy of the imported molecules plus the imported free energy — the latter is also `World.HandEnergy`; a body made of local matter and local decompositions only moves energy between reservoirs and gives no flux); the law `P.EnergyK` ≠ 1 (`energy-k`, see below); the debt of a dead body written off (`write-off`);
- *outputs* (heat): expenditures of actions and life (`dissipate`); the part of expenditures beyond what the body had — it went nowhere (`unpaid`); reaction heat shed by a cooling body (`shed`); what the body held at death (`death`); spontaneous decomposition in the body, as well as photodamage and wear of body matter (`body decay`, see "Wear of body matter"), and on the ground (`loose decay`); pressure reactions in burials (`pressure`); energy of a failed birth (`stillborn`: failed mating and abiogenesis); float rounding (`rounding`, see below);
- *outside the budget*: impact heat of a falling block (`impact`, this is gravity, not chemistry) and the energy abiogenesis extracted from local molecules (`abiogenesis`, an internal transfer — for statistics).

**Check**: `stock(t1) − stock(t0) == inputs − outputs` between two completed ticks (`EnergyAudit.Drift`). Tolerance is 1 + 5·10⁻⁶ of the total flux (`EnergyAudit.Tolerance`). With `World.TrackHeat` (enabled by `--audit`) the fluxes are also checked against the heat that actually passed through `heatIn` (`HeatMismatch`): this catches an unaccounted write into `heatIn`.

**Debt.** `Dissipate` sends into the cell only what the body had; its energy may then become negative. The missing part is recorded in `unpaid` — it left the stock but did not become heat. If the body dies in debt, its negative energy leaves the stock, and this is recorded as `write-off`. For a body that died in debt, `unpaid` and `write-off` cancel each other. Debt that the body managed to work off (or received through a link) is destroyed, not created.

**Rounding.** `Agent.Energy` and `HeatHeld` are double (since save version 9; previously float: an expenditure of 0.001 from an energy of ~30 was rounded by several 10⁻⁷, and the budget converged only to ~0.005–0.02 per 10⁸ of energy passed). `Dissipate`, `Release` (reaction energy) and heat shedding record how much the double actually changed; the difference from the nominal value of `Dissipate` and `Release` goes into the `rounding` flux (now ~10⁻¹⁶ of the stock). Only transfers between bodies (link, division, mating, `share`) remain unaccounted: the amount taken from one and added to the other are each rounded in their own double.

**`EnergyK`.** A reaction in a body yields (or takes) `EnergyK ×` the change in bond energy. At the default of 1 this is exactly the bond change. If the law is changed, the difference is created or destroyed by the law itself: `Bind` and `SplitMol` record `number of turnovers × (de − bond change)` into the `energy-k` input (of either sign). At 1 the flux is zero and trajectories do not change.

**Laws changed during a run.** Other laws do not affect accounting: the stock is computed from actual molecules and energies, and fluxes are recorded with actual values at the moment of the event. `VoxelSpace` changes `MatCap`, but a volcano records the energy of the molecules it actually placed; `GasExpand`, `Gravity`, strength change volumes, pressures and collapses (a block impact is `impact`, outside the budget); `HeatShare`, expenditures, decomposition are recorded as they happen. Test `laws changed mid-run` (`--self-test`, `--self-test-infra`) changes, in the middle of 1000 ticks, `VoxelSpace` (×1.5 and ×0.6), `GasExpand`, `Gravity`, `HeatShare`, `CostBase`, `DecayK` and `EnergyK` (1.25 and 0.8): atoms and energy converge at every mark, the `energy-k` flux is non-zero.

**A new energy event** (a new path where energy appears, disappears or goes into heat) must be recorded in a flux right here — an existing or a new one (`FlowNames`, inputs before `InputsEnd`, outputs before `OutputsEnd`); flux counters are saved in full (`World.Save.cs`), so changing their number requires a new save version.

**Accounting in the creature phase.** Fluxes accumulate where they occur: each tile has its own counters (`Flows` by `Ctx.Slot`); parallel cell chemistry — per row; pressure — in integers; the main thread — in a separate slot. They are summed on read in a fixed order. Determinism does not depend on threads, there are no locks.

**What has been verified.**
- Probes for spawning custom creatures (test `designs`): from outside (matter and energy) — the budget converges, the `design` flux equals the bond energy of the imported molecules plus `HandEnergy`; matter from outside and local energy; local matter and energy from outside; everything local — the `design` flux does not change.
- Saving: test `save/load` compares the flux counters of the loaded world with the original bit for bit and checks the budget of the loaded world from the original's initial snapshot (stock and fluxes continue across the load).
- `--self-test` probes (`EnergyRegression`): combination (exothermic and endothermic), decomposition and `digest`, photon, motor, uptake, drinking, mining, debt and death in debt, death, division, mating, life (expenditures, heat shedding, decomposition in the body), an entombed body, a collapse onto a body, pressure reactions, decomposition on the ground, sediment, strike, volcano, hand, abiogenesis. In each probe the budget converges and the relevant flux is non-zero.
- Worlds of seeds 1 and 7 (1200 ticks) in self-test: drift 0.003 per 2·10⁵ of energy passed. `--long-test` (seeds 1, 2, 3, 20,000 ticks each, including a boom to 32,000 creatures and 167 thousand sedimentation events): worst energy drift 0.021 at a flux of ~1.2·10⁸, atoms 0.0000.
- Atom drift in booms (`--long-test`, seeds 2 and 3: ~2 atoms per ~10⁸ at 22–34 thousand bodies) was float rounding in fractional pools, not a leak: a probe at each place that writes into a pool explained the entire drift (remainder 0.0000), almost all of it from decomposition of loose matter on the ground (`CellChem`: small fractions added to large heaps of products were rounded down). The pools were converted to fixed point; the worst atom drift in `--long-test` is now 0.0000 (CHANGELOG, entry (7)).
- Pressure reactions almost never happen in a living world: a body needs a pair of molecules with an exothermic combination, and life has already used up such pairs; rock of a single species is crushed. Therefore `--long-test`, a quarter of the way through the run, entombs up to 30 bodies that still have such a pair 30 blocks deep — like a vault. The world continues, and the pressure path is exercised under the budgets inside a real world.

**Findings** (2026-10-06).
- Without life (`--pop 0 --noabio`) the budget converges to 0.002 over 4000 ticks: ground chemistry, volcanoes and strikes are accounted exactly.
- With life, while rounding was not accounted separately, drift grew monotonically: seed 1 — +6.6 by 10,000 ticks (~4·10⁻⁶ of the total flux). This is float rounding in `Agent.Energy`: subtracting 0.001 per instruction from an energy of 16–64 systematically gives ±5·10⁻⁷, the sign depending on the binary exponent. Now it is the `rounding` flux (seed 1: −2.2 over 3000 ticks, bodies kept slightly more than they paid), and the remaining drift is in the thousandths. Rounding gives no advantage: the gain is less than half an ulp, i.e. less than the cost of any action. Fixed in save version 9: `Energy` and `HeatHeld` are double (the trajectories of all worlds changed, see CHANGELOG).
- Heat that, according to the fluxes, went into cells matches what passed through `heatIn` to ~10⁻⁷ (float rounding in `heatIn`).
- No path creating energy from nothing was found.

**Remains an approximation.** Heat is a sink, not a stock: cell temperature relaxes toward the climate, heat does not return to chemistry and is not conserved. Gravitational energy (lifting bodies, moving blocks) is not counted separately: lifting is paid from `Energy` and goes into `dissipate`, a block impact is `impact` outside the budget. Light, water, ice and climate remain fields without energy accounting; so does cave climate (`CaveWarm`, `Tmean`): the heat of bodies under a roof warms the cavity air instead of the surface, but enters `heatIn` and the fluxes as before. VM expenditures on a dead body (`unpaid`) are a sink without heat.

## Evolution metrics (`EvoMetrics.cs`)

Computed on demand from living bodies, between ticks. For 20 thousand bodies this takes tens of ms, so they can be computed every ~1000 ticks. Bench prints them at each `--every` mark (the `evo:` line) and writes them to `--log` and to batch CSVs. The tracker remembers previous marks: lineage turnover and novelty are computed between them.
- `genomes` — distinct genomes (`Agent.Hash`); `kin_clusters` — groups of `Agent.Tag` fingerprints (SimHash): each fingerprint, from most to least frequent, joins the first group whose founder is no further than `KinRadius` = 10 bits out of 64.
- `lineages` — living lineages (`Agent.Lineage` — the founder from abiogenesis); `lineage_entropy` H = −Σ p ln p over lineage shares, `lineages_eff` = e^H.
- `dom_share`, `dom_age` — the share of the largest lineage and for how many ticks it has been seen. On first observation the lineage's age is estimated from below — by the age of its oldest body.
- `lineages_lost`, `new_lineages` — lineages that disappeared and appeared since the previous mark.
- `gen_mean`, `gen_max` — generation; `genome_len` — mean genome length; `used_code` — mean fraction of genome bytes with protection > 20 (protection grows for bytes whose command or protein fired; the same as "protected" in the census).
- `specs` — distinct proteins (catalysis kind and targets) with amount ≥ 0.5. `useful_specs` — those of them that name an existing reaction: an allowed combination, decomposition, excitation or motor. `bind_share`…`motor_share` — the fraction of bodies with a protein of each kind.
- `diet_*` — diet shares from `World.Diet` (plant, eater, miner, hunter, idle).
- `new_specs`, `specs_ever` — useful proteins first seen at the mark, and how many have been seen in total. `firsts` — how many materials were broken for the first time with the help of a protein (`World.Firsts`).
- **Where bodies live** (ROADMAP 7). `roofed_share` — share of bodies with ≥ 1 solid block above them (`World.Roof`, voids skipped). Depth of a body = levels below the top of its column (0 on the surface): `body_depth_mean`, `body_depth_p90`, `body_depth_max` over all bodies (the bench's `depth_mean` of `CaveCensus` averages only the roofed ones), `depth_levels_eff` = e^H over the occupied levels (1: all at one depth). `mine_depth` — mean depth below the column's top of the molecules torn out of rock **since the previous sample** (from `World.GeoMined`; `mined_depth_mean` of `GeoCensus` is the cumulative one), `mined_n` — how many; NaN when nothing was mined.
- **Spatial heterogeneity** over 32×32-cell regions (8 × 5, x wraps, rook neighbours; fixed, independent of `--tile`): `pop_moran` — Moran's I of bodies per region over all 40 regions (expectation for a random layout −1/39 ≈ −0.03; > 0 neighbouring regions alike, < 0 a checkerboard); `pop_regions_eff` — e^H of bodies over regions; `diet_moran` — Moran's I of each diet's share over the populated regions (≥ 5 bodies), weighted by the diet's planet share (NaN with < 3 such regions); `diet_beta_rel` — (H(planet) − Σ w_r H(r)) / H(planet), the share of the diet entropy explained by the region (0 all regions alike, 1 one diet per region; the bench's `diet_beta` of `ResCensus` is the same in nats, unnormalised). Diets are `World.Diet` — what actually fed the body lately (EMAs of light, chemistry, mining, attacks), not a role.
- **Oscillation of diet shares** over the last ≤ 48 equally spaced samples (the history restarts when the spacing changes — in the game when the observer doubles its step): for every pair of diets and lag k = 1..8 samples, S = corr(Δa(t), Δb(t+k)) − corr(Δb(t), Δa(t+k)) on first differences of the shares. A Lotka–Volterra cycle (a up → b up → a down) gives a large S; a common driver moving both at once gives S ≈ 0 (the two terms cancel). `osc_score` — the largest |S|, `osc_lead`/`osc_follow` — the diets (codes 0 idle, 1 light, 2 chemistry, 3 soil, 4 hunting), `osc_lag` — the lag in ticks; `osc_p` — (1 + k)/(1 + 199), k of 199 surrogates (each series of changes shuffled in time independently, the same search over pairs and lags) reaching it. `hunt_score`/`hunt_p` — only pairs with the hunters. NaN with fewer than 16 differences: for cycles of a few thousand ticks sample with `--every 100`–`250`. The surrogates use their own fixed-seed `SimRng` (never the world's stream). The shuffled null is lenient: it destroys each series' own rhythm and bursts too (a boom or a crash moves all shares in a few big steps), and in default worlds it rejects in about half the samples. `osc_p_circ`/`hunt_p_circ` — the strict null: each series circularly shifted by its own offset, keeping its rhythm and bursts and destroying only the timing between series; it cannot tell a coupled cycle from two independent ones with the same period (both show a lag), so even a clean periodic pair is not rejected by it. Trust a cycle when both are small and `osc_lag` and the leader stay the same over successive windows.
- The self-test (`--self-test-evolution`) samples them every 50 ticks on one of two identical worlds and requires the same state hash; it also checks the statistic on made-up series (a lagged pair: p ≤ 0.02; noise: p > 0.05) and Moran's I (clump > 0.3, checkerboard −1).

**Game.** `SimObserver` (`src/SimObserver.cs`, owned by `SimRunner`) samples them on the world thread after the tick every 500 ticks (past 2048 samples every other one is dropped and the step doubles) together with the size of every lineage, and publishes immutable `MetricsView`/`LineageView`. The "Metrics" window (F12, `src/Ui/MetricsWindow.cs`) draws chosen columns (and columns of `World.Progress`) over time and exports the table as CSV. This history is not saved: it starts when the world is created or loaded.

Approximations: novelty and lineage turnover are visible only at marks; short-lived proteins and lineages between marks go unnoticed. Lineage age is a lower bound.

## Run batches and comparison

A single run per seed is noise: the same seed in different versions gave anywhere from 36 thousand to 116 creatures. Constants are decided on batches (`tools/bench/Batch.cs`, flags are in the [README](../README.md)):
- `--batch` runs each (seed, replicate) as a separate process, all with the same laws: the flags `--preset`, `--set`/`--param`, `--param-at` (and `--tile`) are passed to each process as is, and the `params` column in `runs.csv` describes them. Laws are set via `ParamRegistry` (`tools/bench/ParamHook.cs`): names from `--list-params`, the value is clamped to the range, an unknown name is an error before the batch starts. Replicate r > 0 is the same seed with `WorldSettings.LifeSeed = r`: only the first bodies (genomes, positions) and the tiles' random streams change (`SimRng` with a different seed; at 0 the streams are the original ones). Elements, relief, volcanoes, water and primordial remains stay the same. Replicate 0 is the seed's original world. It writes `runs.csv` (a row per run and mark), `summary.csv` (median, quartiles, min/max of each column at each mark over seeds and all runs, fractions of extinctions and booms) and a table.
- `--compare a/runs.csv b/runs.csv` compares distributions at the last common mark (population also at the run's quartiles). It outputs medians and quartiles, the difference of medians with a 95% bootstrap interval, the Mann–Whitney U test and a sign test over seeds (medians of replicates; seeds are shared, so this is a paired comparison). For fractions of extinctions and booms — Fisher's exact test. There are many metrics: roughly one in twenty will pass p < 0.05 by chance.
- `ms/tick` in a batch is for comparing batches launched the same way. The processes share the machine; it is not suitable for profiling.
- **Many cores and machines** (ROADMAP 10.5). `--jobs` defaults to the performance cores (macOS `sysctl hw.perflevel0.physicalcpu`: 4 on an M4; elsewhere the logical processors). `--threads T` (default 1) gives every run `DOTNET_PROCESSOR_COUNT=T`: each world steps on T threads (`World.Step` takes its workers from `Environment.ProcessorCount`; 0 — all cores); one single-threaded world per core is the best batch throughput, and the trajectory does not depend on the thread count. A finished run leaves `runs/sS_rR.done` with the batch's signature (ticks, every, laws, pop, tile, code version, server GC); `--resume` skips the runs whose `.done` matches and makes the rest — after a crash, Ctrl-C or a failed run the queue is just "runs without `.done`" (a run counts as finished when it exited cleanly and its CSV reached `--ticks`). `--shard K/N` keeps every N-th run starting at the K-th, to split one batch over N machines. Every row carries `machine` (`--machine`, default the host name) and `code_version` (git commit, `-dirty` with uncommitted tracked changes, `unknown` outside a repository); `batch.txt` in the output directory records the batch. `--merge outdir a b …` (batch directories or `runs.csv` files) matches columns by name, keeps a (seed, rep, laws, tick) row once and reports duplicates that disagree on the state hash (same code and laws must give the same world on every machine), then writes `runs.csv`, `summary.csv` and the table.
- **Speed baseline** (`--perf-baseline`, ROADMAP 10.7): record the median of three repeats — `--perf-baseline --repeat 3 --csv perf.csv --label …` — with the device, load average, fixture seed and tick, ticks, population, end hash and thread count; a spread above ~10 % means a busy machine.
- **Punctuated equilibrium** (`--punctuated`, `tools/bench/Punctuated.cs`, ROADMAP 9.4): from batch directories, onsets of ice ages, volcanic winters, megaeruptions and player catastrophes (`*.events.csv`), a window of `--window-days` (4) after each (merged when they overlap), ticks before `--burn` (2000) left out. Tempo series: the chronicle's speciations, extinctions, new dominants, their sum, and runs.csv columns (`new_lineages`, `lineages_lost`, counted at their checkpoint). Within runs: rate inside vs outside the windows, Wilcoxon signed-rank of per-run differences (exact up to 30 runs), sign test, and a permutation test shifting all onsets of a run by one random offset (one-sided). With `--control dirs` the treated run's windows are laid on the control run of the same seed and rep (same world without the catastrophe): paired Wilcoxon, sign test and an exact sign-flip test.

## Performance and threads

Tick order: sky → gas diffusion → chemistry/climate (every `EnvEvery`) → erosion → volcanoes → strikes → support (adaptive) → metamorphism (every `MetamorphEvery`) → tile assignment → **creatures** → merging of creature-phase results → `Relieve` → abiogenesis. Each stage is timed (`World.Detail`, names in `DetailNames`), see "Profiling" below.

**Creatures run in parallel, by tile.** The map is divided into tiles with side `TileSize` (32 by default: 8×5 tiles), coloured with period 1 + ⌈`SafeGap`/`TileSize`⌉ in x and in y (for 32 — 2×2, four phases). In each phase all tiles of one colour run in parallel. Rationale:
- everything a creature writes during a tick lies within ~7 cells of its starting cell (its own footprint up to 2, a partner within `Near` ≤ 1+2+2, the partner's footprint, one movement step);
- `look` reads up to 16 cells;
- tiles of the same colour are separated by at least `SafeGap` = 32 cells (period − 1 tiles between them) in x or y: 32 > 7 + 16, so one tile's writes fall into neither the writes nor the reads of another. The world wraps around in x, so the number of tile columns is a multiple of the period (widths are distributed evenly, some one cell wider). The `World` constructor checks the separation and throws an exception if the layout violates it.
- The order of creatures within a tile is shuffled at the start of its step by the tile's own `Rng` (previously by a shared generator on the main thread). The tiles of a phase are handed out to threads one by one, most populated first; which thread took a tile does not affect the result.
- `--tile 16` was tested (tiles ~17×16, 3×3 = 9 phases): no faster in a boom, the slowest tile is still almost the entire hotspot, and there are more phases. Hence 32 by default.

**Contract for code that runs in the creature phase (`Live`, a life model's `Think` — model 1: VM commands — the body physics and everything they call):**
- Write only to cells and voxels within this radius. If a new mechanic acts further (a long-range attack, teleport, a global network), it must be deferred: collected in `Ctx` and applied after the phase — or `Tile` must be increased and the rationale revisited.
- Do not write to shared collections directly:
  - marking for the support solver — only `MarkDirty(cell)`;
  - newborns — `Ctx.Newborn`, via `Born`;
  - discoveries — `Ctx.Firsts`;
  - energy fluxes — `Flows[...]` (the slot of one's own tile; see "Energy" in "Budgets"). Any new write into `heatIn` or change to molecular energy outside the existing paths must get its own flux, otherwise `--self-test` and `--audit` will show drift;
  - chronicle — `Ctx.Chron` (proposed "firsts"), `Ctx.DepthAgent`, `Ctx.TrackedDeaths`; biography — only into one's own body (`BioNote`), see "Chronicle";
  - course of evolution — a newborn receives references to the parent and its components into its own body (`EvoBirth` from `Born`); births and deaths get into shared structures after the phase (`EvoTick`), see "Course of evolution";
  - ids — only `NewId()` (64-bit: a tile with tens of millions of births will not overflow);
  - breaking a link with a partner who is no longer nearby (or has died) — `Ctx.Unlinks`: its link list is edited after the phase, in tile order.
- Counters incremented by several tiles — via `Interlocked`.
- `mixtures` and `Buried` are `ConcurrentDictionary`, but a given key is touched by only one tile. Set and clear the `sparse[v]` flags together with the dictionary change (`SetMixture`, `TakeMixture`, `BurialAt`, `MoveBurial`).
- Random numbers — only `Rng` (the tile's stream, `SimRng`). Otherwise determinism is lost, and `--self-test` checks it. Do not use `System.Random` in the core: its state cannot be saved.
- Iterate over burials and compute any floating-point sums over global collections in a fixed order (`ElementBudget` sorts the keys).

The parallelism bottleneck is hotspots: in a boom more than half of all creatures may live in one tile, and their interactions cannot be separated spatially. `AgentBusy` and `AgentLongest` in bench show the total work and the slowest tile of each phase, `PhaseBusy`/`PhaseLongest` — the same per colour.

**Sequential passes over all creatures** (tile assignment, removal of the dead, `SettleAgent` and body weights in support, finding overfull floors in `Relieve`) read bodies in parallel. Everything that changes the world (settling that may kill, summing loads, shifts) is done in the order of `Agents` or of cells, as before: the trajectory is the same.

**Support is woken only when needed.** The solver runs once every `StructureEvery × clamp(1 + voxels/StructureVoxels, 1, StructureStretch)` ticks (voxels: hanging voxels the last pass visited; defaults 5000 and 16: from 4 to 64). This is the solver's resolution in time, not a law of matter; both numbers are in the law registry. It processes only columns marked `structuralDirty` and the overhangs connected to them:
- `TerrainChanged` (geometry changed) always marks;
- `Weakened` (partial mining), `MassChanged` (a little matter in a cave, adsorbed gas) and body weight (`LoadMatters`) mark only if the block is hanging, holds a hanging one (`InSupportPath`) or is close to its compressive limit;
- a decrease in load does not wake the solver;
- growth of lattice order only resets the strength cache: rock only gets stronger from it.

Overhangs are stored in the `overhang[]` array with a per-column counter. If a new change can weaken rock, at least one of these conditions must go through `MarkDirty`.

- Solver state (`hParent`, `hSupport`, `hCarried`, `hEdge`) is stored by voxel index in `activeHanging` — an insertion-ordered set (open addressing), not dense N·Z arrays (that was 126 MB for a few thousand voxels). The traversal order is the same as that of the former `HashSet`, so with equal paths the same parent is chosen.
- Body weight (`bodyLoad`) is stored per cell: one floor per cell in arrays, other floors of the same cell in a dictionary.
- `RefreshColumn` recomputes support from below (`grounded`, `overhang`) only when geometry changes: `topologyVersion` increments in `TerrainChanged`. `ColumnVersion` also increments on partial mining and top-up — this is for the view.
- **Metamorphism** visits only columns with the `annealable` flag. It is set by `TerrainChanged`, `RefreshColumn` and the solver (pressure and order change there), and cleared by the pass itself if no block under pressure with order below 245 remains in the column. The pass is parallel by row in two steps: every block's case is judged on the untouched state (its confinement looks into neighbouring rows), then the judged blocks pack; a neighbour's strength is read without filling its caches or its burial's statistics (otherwise thread timing changed the outcome). Untouched rock that has reached its order is no longer scanned.

**Other.**
- Climate and cell-chemistry fields are computed by parallel row passes. Gas diffusion is one linear pass with two buffers.
- The genome kinship fingerprint (`Agent.Tag`) is lazy; `SimHash` is bitwise, a test checks it against the former voting.
- Diagnostics: `World.Detail`, `DirtBy`, `AgentBusy/AgentLongest`, `PhaseBusy/PhaseLongest`, `AgentAllocated`, the bench flag `--ops` (time per VM command).
- **Game (Godot).** `World.Step` runs on a separate thread (`SimRunner`) that owns the world. Everything that changes the world from the game (brush, N, strikes, abiogenesis, pause, step, fast-forward, new world) is a command applied between ticks, so the trajectory is the same as when stepping from the main thread. The view reads a published snapshot of creatures (`SimFrame`, three rotating buffers) and the relief arrays directly (a read race on numbers is only cosmetic). Methods that write world caches (`CompressionCapacity`, `VoxelCohesion`, `VoxelMass`) and traversals of cell lists (`FloorFill`) are not called by the view: the world thread computes them into the snapshot. New view code must follow the same rule.

**Profiling.** `World.Detail[k]` accumulates ms per stage: `climate`, `cellchem`, `settle/weather` (chemistry and climate), `body loads`, `region`, `solve`, `failures`, `settle agents` (support), `sky`, `diffusion`, `erosion`, `vents`, `strikes`, `metamorph` (columns), `burials` (burials), `tile sort`, `agents`, `merge`, `relieve`, `alarms`, `abiogenesis`, `chronicle` (merging chronicle proposals and surveys), `evolution` (shadows, phylogeny, course-of-evolution measurements). Bench prints them all, memory allocation (total and in the creature phase), the number of gen0/1/2 collections and the share of GC pauses, as well as the world state hash at each mark (`hash`): the same hash means the same trajectory. `--log path.csv` writes a row per `--every` interval: tick, population, births, deaths, ms/tick, all stages, work and the slowest tile per phase, MB/tick, collections, heap, chronicle events (total, important) and fossils. `--tile N` changes the tile size (for comparisons).

**Memory.** Dense per-voxel arrays (7.86 million): `Mat`, `Order`, `sparse`, `overhang` one byte each, `Units` 2 bytes, `Pressure`, `compressionCache`, `cohesionCache` 4 each — ~141 MB (it was ~267 MB together with the solver arrays). 61% of voxels are air (mean height ~75 of 192). Block storage would save up to ~85 MB, but the voxel address `c·Z + z` is the key in all dictionaries (mixtures, burials, loads), and the view reads `Mat`, `Pressure`, `Order` directly. The rework would touch almost every core and view file for the sake of memory, not time: air is not traversed anyway (metamorphism and support go only up to `Height` and only over marked columns). Not done.
- **GPU.** The project uses GL Compatibility; Godot 4.3 compute shaders require RenderingDevice and Forward+/Mobile ([documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.3/tutorials/shaders/compute_shaders.html)). The fields that could be moved take <1 ms per tick, and exchanging data with the GPU every tick would eat the gain.
- **Neural cores.** The creature VM is branching and sparse interactions, not a neural network; the Apple Neural Engine is available only through Core ML for inference and does not fit here.
- Possible next steps:
  - pooling body arrays at birth (`Inv`, `Stack`, `OpCount`… ~1 KB per child). Risky: references to the dead are held by the view, `Target`, `lastAttacker`, `Ctx.Unlinks`;
  - incremental recomputation of overhang components while preserving each one's contribution to shared supports — this is checked by the "regional support" regression.

## World laws (parameters)

All tunable `P` quantities are mutable static fields. Only the world's structural quantities remain `const`: `W`, `H`, `ZMax`, `BlockH`, `StackSize`, `MemSize`, `CallDepth`, `MaxCells` — array sizes and geometry; changing them requires a new build and a new world.
- **Registry** (`ParamRegistry`). Each law has a name, a group (Planet, Mechanics, Rock, VM, Upkeep, Proteins, Light, Temperature, Actions, Body, Wear, Volume, Energy, Reproduction, Links, Movement, Environment, Climate, Water, Space, etc.), a short description, a default value, a range, a step and a `Live` flag. Live means it takes effect immediately. Otherwise the law is read only when a world is created (`InitialPop`, `InitLitter`, `SeaShare`): a change applies to the next world.
- Operations: `Get`/`Set` by name (the value is clamped to the range, integers are rounded), `ResetDefaults`, `Snapshot`/`Restore` as a dictionary, `ParamPreset` presets in JSON (`SavePreset`/`LoadPreset`; a preset contains only differences from the defaults), a `Changed` event, a `Published` array (new after each change, safe to read from any thread). Defaults are captured when the `P` class is loaded (`P.Defaults`).
- **Application.** Each change increments `ParamRegistry.Version`. The world checks the version before a tick (`ApplyParamChanges`) and recomputes whatever is derived from the changed laws (`ParamEffect`):
  - `VoxelSpace` → `Chemistry.MatCap` (how many molecules of a species fill a block);
  - `GasExpand` → `Chemistry.BodyVolume` and the volume of every body anew;
  - `Gravity`, `CompressionK`, `TensionK`, `VoxelSpace` → the strength cache is reset, the support solver recomputes all columns (new pressures; a weakened world may collapse at once). Such a full recomputation costs one heavy tick, so the UI should send the value when the slider is released.
  
  Other laws are read at the moment of use; they have no caches.
- **Determinism.** In the game a law is changed by the command `SimRunner.SetParam` between ticks, and `World.SetParam` writes it into `World.ParamLog` (tick, name, value). A trajectory is the seed plus this history. The log and the current laws go into the save. `P` is static: all worlds in a process share the laws (fine for the game). Self-test resets them to defaults before each test set.
- **Speed.** Replacing `const` with fields does not change the trajectory: hashes on seed 1 matched bit for bit. Tick time is within noise (see CHANGELOG).

## New world

`WorldSettings`: `Seed`, `InitialPop` (< 0 — `P.InitialPop`), `Abiogenesis`, `Strikes`, `LifeSeed` (≠ 0 — the same planet, different first bodies and creature streams; see "Batches"), `Params` (a set of laws: null — keep the current ones, otherwise defaults plus those named). Spontaneous generation and strikes are **off** by default: life is started by the player. Both can be toggled during a run (`World.Abiogenesis`, `World.AutoStrikes`; `SimRunner.SetAbiogenesis`/`SetStrikes`). The old constructor `World(seed, pop, abio, lifeSeed)`, used by bench and self-test, leaves strikes on, as before. The world keeps a copy of its settings (`Settings`) and the laws it was created with (`InitialLaws`).

## Random numbers

`SimRng` is xoshiro256** seeded via splitmix64; the state is four 64-bit numbers. It replicates the needed part of the `System.Random` API: `Next()`, `Next(max)`, `Next(min, max)`, `NextDouble()`, `NextSingle()`, `NextBytes()`. Differences: `Next(max ≤ 0)` returns 0, and `Next(min, max ≤ min)` returns `min`, without throwing; a draw is made anyway. The world stream is `new SimRng(seed)`, tile streams are `new SimRng(seed, 1 + k)`; with `LifeSeed` ≠ 0 the tile seed is mixed with it, and the first bodies are born from a separate stream (only in the constructor). Chemistry is still built from the seed with its own `System.Random` (reproducible with a given seed) and is recreated on load. Switching to `SimRng` changed trajectories once. Self-test thresholds did not need to change.

Burials in metamorphism are traversed in voxel index order. The order of a `ConcurrentDictionary` depended on the history of its table, and reaction heat is summed per cell.

## Saving

`World.Save(path|stream, note)`, `World.Load(path|stream)`, `World.ReadInfo(path)` (header without decompression). Call between ticks. Writing goes to a temporary file, which is then renamed.
- **Format (version `World.SaveVersion` = 15: 2 — energy budget, 3 — chronicle block, 4 — exact fractional quantities (`Qty`), 5 — cave climate, 6 — course of evolution, 7 — geochemistry, 8 — sky, 9 — body energy in double, 10 — climate cycles, 11 — canopy stores, 12 — waterways, 13 — pressure between species (chronicle fields), 14 — life model of every body, 15 — mechanics (relief scale, settling, pressing against ledges); versions from `OldestSaveVersion` = 1 are readable).** Uncompressed header: `PRIMSAVE`, version, tick, seed, number of living bodies, time (UTC), note. Then the body in Brotli (quality 1: three times faster than GZip and smaller). Numbers are little-endian, as in memory.
- **Body order:**
  1. settings (JSON), laws at creation, current laws, tile side;

## Custom creatures

- **Genome text** (`GenomeAsm`). One instruction per line:
  - `op` or `op.N` — immediate bits N;
  - `push N`, `label N`, `jmp N`, `jz N`, `jnz N`, `call N` — N from 0 to 3;
  - second values: `photo thrust mate gnaw grow count drink digest hurt btemp photons swim`, `.3` for variant 3; the motor push is called `thrust` here, `swim up`/`swim down`;
  - `lit N`;
  - `enzyme KIND A B t=T q=Q [alt=K]` — a protein gene: kind, molecules, optimum (−15…35.4 °C, step 0.8), desired quality (the closest one the three bytes can give is taken; `alt` chooses the triple explicitly); `enzyme raw b1 b2 b3`;
  - `byte N…` — raw bytes (this is how an operand cut off by the end of the genome is written);
  - comments `;`, `//`, `#`.
  
  Bytes → text → bytes always round-trip, and canonical text also survives the round trip. Errors come with line numbers.
- **Design** (`CreatureDesign`): name, description, genome text, body composition (`Body`: species number 0–31, `gas`, `any` or the name/formula of a molecule of this world), energy, appearance (optional). Stored in JSON; `CreatureLibrary` manages a folder (in the game — `user://creatures`). `CreatureDesign.FromAgent` takes a design from a living creature. Species numbers mean different molecules in different seeds. Only 0, 2, 4, 6 are always single elements, odd numbers are their excited states.
- **Spawning** (`World.SpawnDesign(design, x, y, SpawnOptions)`), between ticks:
  - `MatterSource.Local`: molecules are taken from the loose matter of the cell and its four neighbours and from a soft top block (`VoxelBarrier` < 2, but not its last molecule: the ground does not shift); `any` — the most abundant one, except gas;
  - `MatterSource.Import`: molecules are brought from outside, each atom is recorded in `HandInput`, their bond energy into the `design` input of the energy budget;
  - `EnergySource.Local`: energy comes from exothermic decompositions of loose matter at the same place, the products remain in the world;
  - `EnergySource.Import`: energy is recorded in `HandEnergy` and into the `design` input of the energy budget;
  - if there is not enough room, matter or energy, nothing is taken (checked before any changes);
  - the body gets a new lineage (the id of the first one spawned) or a given one, the `Agent.Designed` flag, and its lineage is remembered in `World.DesignedLineages`. `Count` > 1 scatters bodies within `Radius`.
- **Examples** (`CreatureExamples`): "Leaf" (light on molecule 0, de-excitation 1 → 0, division), "Mole" (gnaws and digests), "Swimmer" (a gas bubble, rare strokes, a motor). Test `designs`: they are spawned from outside (atoms and energy accounted exactly) and from local matter (atoms do not change); bare ground yields nothing and changes nothing; each example lives 600 ticks, the atom budget converges; marks survive saving; living creature → design → the same genome.

## Chronicle

`World.Chronicle` (`Chronicle.cs`, `World.Chronicle.cs`) is **observation only**: the simulation reads nothing from it, it draws no random numbers, labels ("first predator", "feeds on light") describe what has already happened. Test `chronicle` checks that a world in which every body is tracked gives the same `StateHash` as an ordinary one; bench hashes (seeds 1 and 3, ticks 1000/2000/3000) matched bit for bit with the version without the chronicle.

- **Events** `ChronicleEvent { Seq, Tick, Type, AgentId, Lineage, X, Y, Z, Text, Value, Important, Genome }`. Types `EvType`: `FirstEnzyme`, `FirstReaction`, `Discovery`, `NewDiet`, `DepthRecord`, `CaveDweller`, `FirstSwimmer`, `FirstBottom`, `FirstPredator`, `FirstParasite`, `Speciation`, `Extinction`, `NewDominant`, `Record`, `Climate`, `Player`, `ParasiteSpread`. Important ones are always kept, the rest in a ring of the last `P.ChronicleCap` (10,000). New mechanics (flares, eclipses, ice ages and volcanic winters of `World.ClimateCycles`) simply call `World.Add(EvType.Climate, text, body, value, important)` between ticks.
- **Collection in the creature phase.** A body only proposes: `Propose` puts a candidate into its tile's `Ctx.Chron` (a repeated key within the same tile is discarded), the deepest body of the tile into `Ctx.DepthAgent`, dead tracked bodies into `Ctx.TrackedDeaths`. `Chronicle.Seen` (what has already happened) is only read during the phase. After the phase `ChronMerge` goes through the tiles in order: the first candidate for a key becomes the event, the others are discarded. Therefore the chronicle is the same under any distribution of tiles across threads (a test compares two runs).
- **Who can be "first".** Only a body of a lineage that had ≥ 10 individuals at the last survey (`Agent.Established`, inherited by the child): the random code of a fresh genome does a little of everything. A protein — when its amount has reached 3 and its gene has already "fired" (protected bytes); key — (kind, target molecule). A reaction — carried out by a protein, key — (kind, molecules).
- **Between ticks:** orbital strikes (`Climate`, number irradiated), a new volcano, spawning a design (`Player`, important), a law change (`Player`), saving and loading (`Player`, from `SimRunner`), rock discoveries from `Firsts`.
- **Surveys** every `SurveyEvery` = 500 ticks (the `chronicle` profile stage): lineages of ≥ 10 individuals are remembered (peak, first tick, representative — the oldest, its `Fossil` snapshot); an extinct lineage with a peak ≥ 30, or one that was the largest — `Extinction` (the representative's snapshot becomes a fossil); a new largest lineage, if it is ≥ 20 and a quarter larger than the previous one — `NewDominant`; the dominant diet of a lineage of ≥ 40 individuals (light / chemistry / rock / hunting / mixed / almost nothing, by `World.Diet`) has changed and held for 3 surveys in a row — `NewDiet`; records of mass, age, number of children, genome length — when one and a half times above the last announced one (with minimums). Every fourth survey — `Speciation`: the kinship fingerprints (`Agent.Tag`) of a lineage of ≥ 40 individuals are split into two clusters (centres by bit voting, 4 rounds); a split is declared if both are ≥ 20, the centres are ≥ 16 bits out of 64 apart and 1.8 times further apart than members are from their centres on average. Known branches follow drift; only a branch far from all known ones counts as news. A depth record — with a step of at least a quarter of the previous one and at marks of 3, 10, 30 levels.
- **Biography** (`Agent.Bio`, a ring of 64 entries `BioEntry { Tick, Other, Value, Kind, Arg }`) is written only for tracked bodies (`Agent.Tracked`) and only into one's own body in one's own `Live`; the exception is a victim that was attacked (the attacker modifies its body anyway: it is within reach). Entries: birth (parent, partner), put under observation, first protein of each kind, first reaction with a protein, went under a roof and came out, kills, thefts and gifts (1st, 2nd, 4th, 8th…), attacks, irradiation, records, children (1st and every 10th), chronicle events, infection with foreign code, death.
- **Whom to track** (`TrackWhy`): lineage founders (spontaneous generation, N, the initial population), those spawned from the designer and their first-generation children (`P.ChronicleTrackKids`), event participants, those selected by the player (`SimRunner` calls `Track` between ticks). Every body has `ParentId` and `TrackedAncestor` (nearest tracked ancestor) — 16 bytes; the chain of tracked bodies leads to the founder. `Chronicle.Ancestry` nodes exist only for tracked bodies.
- **Fossils** (`Fossil`: genome, body composition, proteins, lineage, parent, birth and death ticks, cause, event, biography, appearance) — for dead tracked bodies, if they took part in events, were selected or spawned by the player, or founded a lineage (or were children of a spawned one) and left descendants, plus representatives of extinct lineages. Limit `P.FossilCap` (1000): the least important is evicted (`ImportanceOf`: event, player, design, founder, log₂ of children, age). Ancestry nodes of the dead without a fossil are forgotten first beyond 8·`FossilCap`. `Fossil.ToDesign` gives a `CreatureDesign` (genome as text, molecules, appearance; if there are fewer molecules than `MinBody`, `any` is added).
- **Game.** `SimRunner` publishes a `ChronicleView` (a copy of events, counters, fossils and ancestry) no more than 4 times per second when the chronicle has changed, and in `SimFrame` — the biography of the selected body, its living parent and children (searched every 0.5 s) and its mass history. The main thread does not read world collections.
- **Cost.** Hooks in the creature phase are flag checks and reads of `Seen`; surveys and merging are the `chronicle` stage. Measurement in the CHANGELOG.

## Course of evolution

`World.Shadow` (`Evolution.cs`), `World.Phylo` (`Phylogeny.cs`), `World.Progress` and the hooks (`World.Evolution.cs`) are **observation only**, like the chronicle: the simulation does not read them, random numbers come from their own streams (`SimRng` of the shadows and pair sampling), labels describe what has already happened. Bench hashes (seeds 1 and 3, ticks 1000/2000/3000) matched bit for bit with the version without them; test `evolution` compares a world with measurements every 10 ticks and one without measurements.

- **Collection.** In the creature phase `Born` calls `EvoBirth`: the child receives references to the parent and its components (and their ids), the size of the genome edit (`EditSize`: the number of differing bytes at equal length, otherwise the changed middle without the common prefix and suffix) and its own components. Only the new body is written. After the phase, births are taken in merge order (tiles in order, then the main thread), deaths in the order in which `RemoveDead` finds them. Founders (spontaneous generation, N, the first bodies, those spawned) enter immediately, as roots. O(births + deaths) per tick. The world side (component ids `Agent.EvoIds`, carriers, activity) is computed on the world thread; both shadows and the tree — by a background task in the same order while the next tick runs. It is awaited (`EvoJoin`) by the next merge, a measurement, spawning a founder and saving; tests and external reads use `EvoSettle`. The task touches only the shadows, the tree and the `Agent.Taxon` fields, which are read by neither the simulation nor the view; the newborn's genome (hash) is captured at merge.
- **Components** of a body are fixed at birth from code that proved useful to its lineage: bytes with `Prot > 0` (protection grows when a command or protein fired; a child inherits it × 0.9 rounded down, so "used" means fired in the parent or grandparent). A component is a protein specification (catalysis kind and targets of the gene of a used `enzyme` command) or a motif: a hash of a used byte and the three following it (operands and what it passes to), wherever it stands. Sets identical to the parent's are one shared array. A component's activity is the Σ over ticks of the number of living carriers.
- **Neutral shadow** (Bedau): the same births and deaths, but the parent and the deceased are random, the world is not simulated. A shadow child copies the components of a random shadow body and changes the same way a real child differs from its real parent: it loses each component with probability equal to the loss fraction among real children (a running value over ~1000 births), gains as many entirely new ones (never seen in the world) and as many already existing ones (a copy of a component of a random shadow body) as the real one. There are two shadows: the **reference** shadow sets the threshold θ — the 95th percentile of the activity of its living components at the measurement; the **control** shadow and the world are counted against θ identically: a component that exceeds θ for the first time is counted once. **Novelty** = world − control. A shadow counted against its own percentile is biased low (its top 5% are what sets θ), hence the second one. Accounting for losses by fraction rather than by count, and one sample per loss, were verified with synthetic data: with random births and deaths novelty is around zero (8 on average with ~84 adaptive), with selection for "good" components — 73–138.
- **Phylogeny** — as in Empirical's systematics: taxon = genome (`Agent.Hash`); a child with the parent's taxon genome joins it, otherwise it founds a new one beneath it. A taxon with no living members and no descendants is removed immediately (and up the tree); at each measurement `Compact` cuts out dead taxa with a single descendant (node depth `Depth` is the original number of steps from the root, it does not change). After `Compact` there are no more than two nodes per living taxon. Node ids are persistent (`Agent.Taxon`), freed ones are reused.
- **Tracks** (`EvolutionHistory.Names`, every `P.ProgressEvery` = 200 ticks; history up to 1024 measurements, when full every second one is kept and the step doubles):
  - novelty: `adaptive` (world), `adaptive_shadow` (control), `novelty`, `adaptive_now` (living above θ), `comps`, `comps_shadow`, `theta`, `carry`/`carry_shadow` (components per body);
  - complexity: `code_used` (bytes with protection > 0 per body), `body_proteins` and `body_reactions` (distinct proteins with amount ≥ 0.5 and those of them naming an existing reaction), `body_mass`, `body_cells`;
  - ecology: `niches` (combinations of `World.Diet` diet × depth band — surface, 1–2 below the surface, ≥ 3, in water — × 32×32 tile with ≥ 5 bodies), `diet_div` (e^H over diet), `under_share`, `water_share`, `lineages_big` (lineages ≥ 10 bodies);
  - pace (cumulative; rate is the difference between measurements): `dom_changes` (changes of the largest lineage), `speciations`, `extinctions` (from the chronicle); `dom_drift` — the number of bits out of 64 by which the `Tag` fingerprint of the most frequent genome moved since the last measurement;
  - phylogenetics: `mrca_age` (ticks since the MRCA of the living members of the largest lineage: its root after `Compact`; with several roots there is no common ancestor since founding, and the oldest founder is taken), `pair_dist` (mean distance in mutation steps over 128 random pairs of the living), `phylo_div` (sum of branch lengths, Faith's phylogenetic diversity in steps), `phylo_taxa` (living genotypes), `phylo_branches`, `phylo_roots`, `dom_depth` and `dom_muts` (lineage length and accumulated changed bytes of the most frequent genotype), `phylo_nodes`;
  - `progress_index` — **the summary hint**: for novelty, `code_used`, `niches` and `pair_dist` — the change of their least-squares line over the last `P.ProgressWindow` ticks relative to the mean absolute value (within ±1); the median of the four. ≥ 0.05 — "progressing", ≤ −0.05 — "regressing", otherwise "stagnating". It is a hint: population growth raises three of the four tracks.
- **Game.** `SimRunner` publishes an `EvolutionView` (a copy of the list of measurements — the arrays themselves do not change after creation — and the index) after each new measurement. The "Course of evolution" window (F10, `src/Ui/EvolutionWindow.cs`); the "Tree of life" window (F1, `src/Ui/TreeWindow.cs`) and the "ranges of clades" overlay (`View3D.RangeOverlay`).
- **Bench.** The `progress:` line at each mark, columns in `--log`, `runs.csv`, `summary.csv`, `--compare`.

**Approximations.** Components that went extinct and returned are counted as new. The shadow is panmictic: in the world lineages and locations are separated, so in a boom, when fresh components appear by the thousands and die together with local outbreaks, the control shadow may overtake the world — novelty goes negative (seed 3 in a boom after 9000 ticks: −57 at 10,000, −407 at 14,000). The distance between individuals is in genotype steps, not by code alignment; `dom_muts` is an estimate of edit size. Food-chain length and a tournament against ancestors (7.1) are not done.

- **Cladogram and ranges** (game). While the "Tree of life" window (F1) or the "ranges of clades" overlay (M) is shown, `SimObserver.BuildTree` runs on the world thread between ticks at most once a second: after `EvoSettle` (which only joins the background job) it sums living bodies per subtree of `World.Phylo` and keeps the clades with at least a chosen share of the population (default 1 %, at least 3 bodies; raised until ≤ 90 branches). A chain of single significant children is folded into one branch: it starts at the genotype where it split off (`Phylogeny.Origin`) and lasts until now, its children are the significant clades of its first branch point. Every body is assigned to its deepest branch; the oldest one is the branch's representative, and the cells they stand in make the range map. It does not call `Compact` and draws no random numbers. Checked: two worlds (seeds 3 and 7, 6000 ticks, the second observed — metrics every 500 ticks, a tree every 50) gave the same `StateHash` and the same `World.Progress` columns at every 1000 ticks; a tree of ~9000 living genotypes takes ~2 ms, a metrics sample ~60 ms at ~10 000 bodies. Approximations: only living clades are in the tree (dead branches are pruned by `Phylogeny`); extinct lineages are seen in "Lineages over time" (session samples) and in the chronicle; clade identity across rebuilds is (start genome, origin tick); the range shows one branch per cell (the last body found there), with a two-cell halo.

## Population templates

A population template (`PopulationTemplate`, `Population.cs`) is a copy of real bodies of a world, not a recipe: in the game it is made in the "Library of life" window (U) from the selected creature's lineage, the clade picked in the tree of life (F1) or every body in a circle of brush 5, and stored as a JSON file in `user://populations` (the window shows the path; files can be copied between machines and imported/exported).
- **Copy** (`World.CopyPopulation(bodies, name, source)`, between ticks, changes nothing). Per body: genome bytes and per-byte protection (`Prot`, base64, exact), whole molecules (`Body`), partly absorbed matter (`Pend`, raw `Qty`), proteins (kind, A, B, optimum, quality, amount, material, folded matter as raw `Qty`, gene position), free energy, looks (hue, saturation, brightness, scale, shape), position relative to the mean position of the group (x wraps), its floor against its column top (`Rise`) and against the anchor column (`Floor`), swimming height (`Lift`), source id, lineage, parent and generation, the `Designed` mark. Choosing bodies: `LineageBodies`, `CladeBodies(hash, origin)` (the taxon of `World.Phylo` with that genome hash and origin and everything below it), `AreaBodies(x, y, r)`.
- **Chemistry signature** (`ChemSignature`): the source seed, element masses and all 32 species with formula (atoms per element), excitation, mass, bond energy, bond strength, solidity and the gas role.
- **Paste** (`World.PastePopulation(t, x, y, PasteOptions)`, between ticks): the anchor goes to (x, y), each body to its own offset, in order of source id (parents first), each independently, on the top floor of its column (a cave position is not reproduced; the swimming height is kept as far as the water reaches). Matter and energy as for designs: `MatterSource.Local` — loose matter of the cell and its four neighbours (fractional amounts exactly in `Qty`), then whole molecules of the soft top block (never its last one; the unneeded part of a block molecule stays loose in the cell); `Import` — atoms booked in `HandInput`, bond energy in the `design` input; energy `Local` from exothermic splits of loose matter there (products stay), `Import` booked in `HandEnergy` and `design`. A body the place cannot supply is not made and nothing is taken for it; reasons are counted in `PasteResult.Failures`. Pasted bodies keep their genomes, get `Agent.Designed`, their lineages enter `DesignedLineages` under the template name (the planted marker in 3D), a chronicle event (`Player`, text in both languages) and tracking. With `KeepRelations` the bodies of one source lineage share a new lineage (the id of the first pasted), keep their generation, and a child whose parent was pasted gets `ParentId` and is registered under the parent's taxon in the tree; without it, one new lineage for all, generation 0, all founders. No new per-body state: save format unchanged (version 10).
- **Another chemistry** (`ChemMap`): if every species of the signature equals this world's, the paste is exact. Otherwise each template species goes to the nearest species here: the same formula and excitation when it exists (atoms per element kept), else by a distance over composition, excitation, gas role, mass, bond energy, bond strength and solidity; several may land on one. Molecules, partial matter, protein materials and protein targets (A, B) are mapped; the body is made of this world's species (local or brought in and booked as such), so element conservation holds in the target world. Genomes are kept byte for byte by default, except protein genes when `RemapGenes` is on: the low 5 bits of the A/B bytes of each `enzyme` instruction met in a straight read (like the disassembler) are replaced by the mapped species (the quality of such a gene changes with its bytes). Species numbers the code itself puts on the stack (`push`, `lit`), and code reached only by jumping into the middle of an instruction, are not remapped — the window and the bench warn about it and list every changed species.
- **Test** `population` (`--self-test`, separately `--self-test-population`): a lineage with proteins and partial matter of a living world → JSON → file → load gives the same JSON; pasted from outside half a planet away, every body's genome, protection bytes, molecules, partial matter, proteins, energy, looks, mass and volume equal its source; the atoms booked equal the bodies' atoms, the imported energy is booked, the element budget and the energy ledger close; one body from local matter and local reactions books nothing from outside and closes both budgets; a bare spot makes nothing and changes nothing; save/load keeps the pasted bodies and lineages; pasted into seed 2 every species with its formula present keeps it, budgets close.
- **Limits:** a template stores a body's matter and proteins, not its VM state (stack, memory, instruction pointer start from zero), age, counters, links or biography; big bodies start on one cell and grow back by their mass; floors are the top surface of the target columns.

## Game commands (`SimRunner`)

Everything is applied by the world thread between ticks. The result arrives in an optional callback (on the world thread) and as a line in `Notices` (a failure starts with `BadNotice`; windows show them as pop-up messages). Lineage names of custom creatures for the panel are a copy of `DesignedLineages`, updated after spawning and after a world change.
- **Laws:** `SetParam`, `ResetParams`, `ApplyPreset`, `LoadPreset`, `SavePreset`; reading from the main thread — `Params`, `ParamGroups`, `ParamValues`, `ParamVersion`.
- **World:** `SetAbiogenesis`, `SetStrikes`; `NewWorld(settings, warm)`; `Save(path, note)`; `Load(path)` (replaces the world, like a new one); `ReadSaveInfo`.
- **Creatures:** `SpawnDesign(design, x, y, options)`; `CopyPopulation(select, name, source, done)`, `PastePopulation(template, x, y, options, done)` (population templates).
- **Regions:** through `Do`: `World.CopyRegion`, `World.PasteRegion` (the regions window posts the result back to the main thread).
- **Catastrophes:** `Catastrophe(c, done)` — `World.Catastrophe` between ticks (chronicle, law log, chronicle publication).
- **Autosave:** `AutosaveMinutes`/`AutosaveDir`/`AutosaveSlots`, result — `LastAutosave`.

Replacing the world increments `WorldGeneration`. Based on it, `Main` calls `View.SetWorld`.

## Limits of the approximation

- 4 elements, 16 formulas × 2 states; arbitrary new chemical species are not added during play.
- The lattice is a scalar order and contacts, without atomic coordinates, anisotropy or strain computation.
- One strongest support path per block; an alternative distribution over several paths is not optimised. Conservative failures are possible.
- Falling happens discretely down to the floor within one computation step, without a rigid-body trajectory and without elastic rebound.
- Water, temperature and atmosphere remain column fields; there is no full hydrology or cave ventilation. Cave climate is also per column (roof above the body, annual mean, body heat), without cavity volume and without heat exchange between its columns. Currents come only from the water's own flow (no wind stress, no thermohaline circulation), so open seas are nearly still; caves have no currents. Floating ice does not drift and does not grow from below; ice does not stop evaporation. Cave water: a full run's level is its roof (no pressure head, no siphons), only lateral faces connect (water does not seep through rock or fall through a hole in a roof from the surface water above), cave water neither freezes nor evaporates.
- Dissolved matter is not a separate stock but a fraction of the remains on the bottom: taking it reduces those same remains. Swimmers do not shade lower layers — each catches a photon from the cell's common flux with its own depth-dependent probability.
- Parameters belong to an artificial world, not to real terrestrial rocks or SI units.
